What it is
The Doomsday Clock is a yearly statement by the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists that answers one question in picture form: how close do these people think humanity is to wrecking itself with tools it built?
Midnight = catastrophe from human-made tech (originally nukes; later climate, biotech, AI). The hand is the punchline. Right now it sits at 85 seconds to midnight, set 27 January 2026 — closest it has ever been.
They say it themselves: it is not a forecast. They look at things that already happened and at current trends, then pick a time that they think matches the mood of the risk. Doctor’s diagnosis, not a prophecy.
Where it came from
1945: Manhattan Project scientists, including people around Einstein and Oppenheimer, start the Bulletin because they just helped invent a city-killer and want civilians to notice.
1947: artist Martyl Langsdorf needs a magazine cover. She draws a clock with the hand near midnight because it looked urgent. That doodle became the brand.
First setting: 7 minutes to midnight. Nuclear arms race only.
Who moves the hand
The Science and Security Board — scientists and policy people, consulting a Board of Sponsors that includes Nobel laureates. They meet twice a year; they usually announce in January. They have moved it 27 times since 1947. Not only toward midnight. 1991, after the Cold War thaw and START, they put it at 17 minutes — the furthest it’s ever been. Since about 2010 it has only crept closer.
Scope grew:

1947–2000s: mostly nuclear
2007 onward: climate added
later: biosecurity, “disruptive tech” / AI, and they now talk about misinfo as a multiplier

How to read 85 seconds
Analogy: it’s a dashboard light, not an odometer. “Closer than last year” is the claim they actually stand behind. Comparing 85 seconds in 2026 to 2 minutes in 1953 is apples-to-oranges — different mix of threats, different politics, and they have been using seconds for years so a 4-second move still makes a headline.
2026 reasons they listed: great-power nationalism, collapsing arms-control habits (New START expired that February), Ukraine, Middle East / Iran, climate still getting worse on the metrics they watch, AI and lab-bio without shared rules, “failure of leadership.” Standard Bulletin paragraph, just tighter this year.
The honest caveats

Subjective committee judgment. No equation. Two smart people can look at the same warheads and CO₂ and disagree.
Mixing unlike risks (nuclear winter ≠ slow climate ≠ speculative AI) into one hand is a communication trick, not physics.
Critics call it alarmist theatre / boy-who-cried-wolf. Fair charge if you treat it as a probability. Unfair if you treat it as “these experts want you to look at X this year.”
It tracks their sense of unmanaged danger, not “the end of times” from Revelation. Different machine.

So when I mentioned it earlier: it is evidence that a particular club of nuclear/climate/security people think the human-made risk stack is historically high. It is not evidence that a trumpet sounds on Thursday.
