“Papa Legba, vwalye n'ganga! Louvri chemen, louvri pot. Kimbisa fòs, w'a ban nou limyè. Kongo bonda, dlo lavi.”

Alien.

Watching him.

In the corners.

In mirrors.

The rain did not stop.

The water shimmered with a lattice of glyphs. Not written — remembered. Passed from tongue to tongue, down generations. Echoes of voices drowned long ago. The invocation had not been invented. It had always existed — waiting for someone foolish enough to speak.

And the thing they had awakened… it was not a god.

It was not a spirit.

It was not a curse.

It was language itself, unfurled into a sentience — an ageless echo wrapped in water, words that dreamed of form. A linguistic organism built not of cells, but of phonemes and meaning and moisture.

And it hated silence.

In the last days, the survivors clung to high ground.

Cities became lakes.

Rivers ran backward.

Speakers broadcast nothing but static, save for occasional bursts of the phrase:

“Papa Legba, vwalye n'ganga…”

Some tried to drown themselves. It didn’t work. The water wanted them alive — wanted witnesses.

The UN fell. Satellites blinked out one by one. Communication collapsed.

But still, water moved.

In a mountainside compound in Iceland, a team of linguists tried to isolate the phonetic core. One of them, soaked in sweat and coughing seawater, pointed at a screen covered in waveform analysis and croaked:

“It’s recursive.”

“What is?”

“The phrase. It rewrites itself every time it’s spoken. Adapts. Infects the semantic layers of thought. It’s not understood — it’s embedded.”

And then the floodwaters burst through the reinforced walls like paper.

In the end, Elias and Arthur returned to the same place they had started — their homes, or what remained of them. Walls stripped to bone. Floors warped. Everything damp, even the air. Mold patterns resembled symbols.

They didn’t resist anymore.

They sat. They listened.

And in the water’s whispers, they finally understood.

The words were never a call.

They were an arrival.

The price of utterance was never death. That would be mercy.

The price is becoming a tongue through which the current speaks.
