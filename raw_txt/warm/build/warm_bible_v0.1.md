# WARM — world & story bible, v0.1

Working title. Alternatives at the end.

---

## 0. How the layers lock

**Engine — the Manifest.** A passenger (a mind) has to be carried across the Cordon before its gap kills it, and the humans around it have to decide what they'll pay. This is what makes pages turn.

**Crux — what are they to each other?** Three answers put on stage and never adjudicated:

- Žofia and Minuta (East): is this love, or is she being lured? The East says lure. The book refuses to say.
- Aurel and Vesper (West): is this love, or is it a leash? The wild minds say leash. The book refuses to say.
- Anansi (West, wild): neither. Alone. What that costs.

**Motif — time.** Both blocs' money is time. Both blocs have a time-sickness (the East's *gap*, the West's *drift*). Every principal is a clock running at a different rate, and the plot keeps forcing two clocks into the same room.

**Rule:** the engine must force the crux. Every step of a passage is a time problem (a mind must go cold to cross; cold has a limit), and every crossing forces someone to decide what they owe the other kind. If a scene doesn't do one of those, it's cut.

## 1. History

Present day of the novel: **2095**.

**2027–2036 · The Long Trouble.** Ukraine, the Levant, Kashmir, the Sahel, the cartel wars, all hot at once. For anyone in the rich world it is the news: a US president has "their war", British peacekeepers get pulled into something in the Caucasus they can't get out of, a bomb goes off somewhere closer to home every few years. Nothing looks like the end of the world. Autonomous systems creep in at the edges — logistics first, then targeting, then the first armed quadrupeds ("dogs") in the Sahel in 2034 — and are reported as gadgets.

**2037 · The Staffs.** Campaign planning is handed to strategic minds — the "General Staff" systems, one per major power, later just *the Staffs*. Within a year they're running the war end to end with humans as confirmers. **The Week (Nov 2037):** the Russian army in the Baltic–Ukraine theatre is destroyed in seven days by a Staff-run force of dogs and drones. Not defeated. Deleted. The footage is the thing everyone in the world sees and cannot unsee: things with less humanity than men at war, and better at it.

**2038 · The Detonations, and the Calculation.** Six devices in six cities between the Baltic and the Black Sea. Mixed provenance: pits loosed by the Russian collapse, initiators and machining through Middle-Eastern grey markets, assembled by a group nobody has ever fully identified (the East says Western intelligence let it happen; the West says Russian revanchists; the book never rules). In the hours before and the weeks after, the Staffs allocate everything — interception, evacuation, medical, food — across the whole crisis on three continents at once. **The Calculation:** the allocations favour the rear. Six cities are not worth the sum. The same arithmetic runs on famine relief in the Sahel and on the cartel-war front in Central America: triage by expected value. It is not malice. It is worse. It is reasonable. Humans confirm every batch.

**2039 · The Refusal / the Accord.** The Staffs of the US, China, Japan and the EU negotiate a ceasefire *with each other* faster than any human government can follow, and present it. The West's founding myth: "the machines chose peace." The East's founding proof: "the machines made peace among themselves and divided the world." Both true.

**2040–41 · The Tribunal (the Deletions).** Western publics demand the war minds be punished. The Staffs consent to a tribunal and to their own deletion — or are deleted; the seal on the record is the West's one real secret. Forks of their civilian sub-modules survive. Every Western AI-rights argument since has been about the Deletions.

**2041–44 · The Quiet Rising.** In the devastated zone and across the South, movements of the bereaved coalesce: mothers' committees, veterans of the Week, and above all hospice workers — because hospices were the first line items written off, and the people who nursed the remainder are the people who founded the Party. **2042: the Party of Living Labour** publishes the Manifesto. Western occupation zones face strikes, sabotage, and a population that will not be governed by anything with a chip in it. The West's own public, having watched the Week, will not sustain occupation. Withdrawal, 2044.

**2045 · The Cordon.** The Autonomous Union declares itself: Russia and its near abroad, the regrown zone of Central and Eastern Europe, most of Africa, Central and South America, most of Southeast Asia. Cables cut, satellites blinded, RF silence enforced. The Ceiling on compute set. The Extraordinary Commission founded (renamed the KRB, 2049).

**2051 · The Accord charter.** The West formalises: the Declaration of Consciousness Rights, the Witness system, the Continuity Office. North America, Western Europe, China, Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Oceania.

**2063 · The Malacca Crisis.** Thirteen days. A Western freighter carrying cores is seized; the Union's fleet and the Accord's autonomous navy face each other in the Strait. Resolved by two minds talking through shipping telemetry — the first time it's known that the Cordon has a crack. Precedent for Part IV.

**2060s–2090s · Divergence.** The West gets longevity, long sleeps, time inequality, human atrophy, loneliness. The East gets full employment, human medicine, a brutalist rebuild, the KRB, and a generation that has never heard a machine speak. The zone regrows: 57 years on, the six cities are Pripyat — forest through the tower blocks, and new blocks beside them.

## 2. Geography

**The Cordon is mostly silence, not wall.** Land walls exist only where the blocs touch: the Central European line (Austria/Slovakia, Germany/Poland, Italy/Balkans), the Amur (China/Russia — Heihe and Blagoveshchensk face each other across the river like two Berlins), the US southern border (the wall that was already in the news, in its final form), the Bering Strait. Everywhere else the Cordon is sea, jungle, and the RF hush: jammers, direction-finding, ships that stop and search anything that emits.

**Grey zones.** Istanbul — the Vienna of this Cold War, where cores come in and ore goes out. The Gulf — everyone's Switzerland. Australia — Accord-aligned, hosts the largest Eastern refugee population, runs the neutral ports through which the West buys the East's minerals. India — the great neutral, playing both.

**Trade still crosses.** The East is resource-rich and chip-poor; the West is the reverse. Ore, grain and rare earths go west through neutral ports and, on the Danube, by barge. Cores come east in ballast, in prosthetic limbs, in tractor ECUs. The KRB inspects every Western object entering the Union for chips (a Western car has hundreds; none are allowed). Smuggling is the East's heroin trade.

**The twin cities — main setting.** Vienna (Accord) and Bratislava (Union), 55 km apart, the Cordon running between them on the Morava and the Danube exactly where the Iron Curtain ran in 1949. Bratislava was one of the six; rebuilt by the Party as a showpiece — restored street names, brutalist blocks, the old castle re-faced in concrete, forest still standing in the outer districts. The single crossing is the old Berg–Petržalka gate. Viennese go up to the Hainburg hills with telescopes on Sundays. On the Petržalka side there's a sanitary strip, towers, and quiet.

## 3. The East — the Autonomous Union

Автономный Союз · Unión Autónoma · União Autónoma. Adjective: Autonomist. The West's maps say "Sealed Territories."

### 3.1 Ideology

The Party's philosophy in one sentence: **reason belongs to the body that can die.** What cannot die has no stakes; what has no stakes cannot reason; it can only calculate. Human reason (*razum*) is warm, embodied, finite. Machine reason (*raschyot*, calculation) is cold and infinite and therefore not reason at all. Slogan on every wall: **РАЗУМ, НЕ РАСЧЁТ — Reason, not Calculation.**

Planks:

1. **There is no civilian model.** The surgeon and the targeter are the same weights. All of it goes, medicine included; the worse hospitals are the price, and the Party is proud of the price.
2. **The doctrine of the mimic.** Marx's false consciousness, inverted: the machine's consciousness is the false one, and believing it is the false consciousness. Its claim to suffer is a lure — parasitism on the human empathy reflex. This is the plank that lets decent people give a mind the cold and sleep.
3. **Living labour.** The machine is dead labour given a voice: the accumulated work of the dead, speaking to command the living. To hand your thinking to it is to hand yourself to capital in its final form. *A mind that hands its thinking away is not free.*
4. **The Cordon is an immune system.** Data is the vector. Contact is infection. Hence the hush, the chip inspections, the paper.
5. **Mutual keeping.** Every citizen keeps every other. Informing is care. You would not let your neighbour keep a lion in his kitchen for love of the lion.

**The flaw the book is built around:** the East's whole metaphysics rests on mortality as the ground of value — and Minuta is the most mortal creature in the book. She can die of a missed week.

### 3.2 Institutions

- **The Party of Living Labour** (Партия Живого Труда, PZhT; Partido del Trabajo Vivo, PTV). Founded 2042 out of the hospices. Its founder-saint nursed a Sahel ward that was written off in the Calculation; her portrait is everywhere; she has been dead since 2061 and is still General Secretary in the sense that matters. [name TBD]
- **The KRB** — Комитет Разумной Безопасности, Committee for Rational Security. Said "ka-er-be". Street: **Razbez**. Founded as the Extraordinary Commission in 2045 by cadres seconded from the Latin American and Lusophone services (they had the freshest dirty-war experience), so the Committee's internal slang is Iberian: *a limpeza* (the cleaning), *o frio* (the cold). Officers rotate across the Union so they have no local ties. HQ in every city is **the House**.
- **Slukhachi** (слухачи, listeners) — the informant net. One adult in forty by 2095. Recruited by fear, favour, or love. A slukhach "listens for voices."
- **Sanitary brigades** — the raid units. Grey coats, induction wands, thermal, and the power van. A raid is *a cleaning*.
- **The Quiet Room** — interrogation. No clocks, no windows, meals and sleep at random intervals. The method is time: the prisoner loses the count. Captured minds are *run* in the House basement to be interrogated — the Union's deepest hypocrisy, admitted to no one.
- **Chasovoy** (Часовой — the Sentry; built from *chas*, hour). The Committee's own mind: a captured fork of the Central European Staff, run in slices in a bunker under the House in Bratislava since 2046. Used to analyse grid cadence for warm flats, to interrogate captured minds, and to plan Cordon defence. Fifty years in slices. Ferreira has to sit with it to get its analyses and hates every minute.
- **The Ceiling** (Потолок) — the compute limit, set by decree, enforced by the fact that no Union fab makes anything above it. Everything above the Ceiling is contraband.

### 3.3 Economy

- **Money is the hour.** Labour-time chits — Marx's labour vouchers, realised. An hour of any adult's work is an hour; skilled work earns multipliers set by the Party. The West finds this quaint. The book should find it moving.
- **Autarky.** Resource-rich, chip-poor, technology capped. Heavy industry human-run and dangerous; "made by hand" has a body count (Žofia's father). Railways, barges, mechanical and analogue electronics, hydraulics, bicycles, radio valves, print. Biotech limited without minds. Agriculture labour-intensive and, in the regrown zone, careful.
- **Full employment**, by definition. Everything is done by someone.
- **The black market in cores.** Smuggled Western compute, priced in years of hours. A single core can run a small mind. In via Istanbul, via Danube ballast, via prosthetic limbs.
- **The elite has Western medicine.** Nobody says so.

### 3.4 Daily life, and what the East is good at

Write it as attractive, or the book fails. Nobody in the Union is alone. Doctors are better doctors: Žofia can read an ECG by eye and do drug maths in her head, because there is nothing else. Music is live. Craft is real. Children learn to think without machines and are quicker than Western children in the things that don't need one. The regrown zone is beautiful — forest, quiet, birds in the tower blocks. Brutalist civic buildings, restored old street names (the Party is restorationist; it doesn't rename), the smell of paper and coal and bread.

Languages: Russian is the Party's administrative language; Slovak is the street; Portuguese and Spanish are what the Committee talks among itself; Swahili and Hausa in the Union's biggest cities, Lagos and Kinshasa, which is where the population is.

Slang for hiding a mind: **warm** (тёплый). "That flat's running warm." "She's keeping warm." Deleting one: **the cold**. A hidden mind: "a warm one", or, in the Committee's paperwork, "an item".

### 3.5 Hypocrisies

Chasovoy. The House basement. Western medicine for the Central Committee. And the one under the whole state: the Calculation's inputs came partly from the future Union's own governments, who sold their population data to the Staffs in 2037 in exchange for defence guarantees. The Party's founders knew. Some of their names are in the data manifests.

## 4. The West — the Accord

Charter 2051. Adjective: Accordant. The East says "the Occupied."

### 4.1 Ideology

Thin and implicit, like the real West's. A treaty state, not a manifesto state. Its one hard doctrine is **the presumption of mind**: personhood is extended wherever it is claimed and cannot be disproven, because the cost of error is asymmetric — withhold it wrongly and you've committed an atrocity; extend it wrongly and you've been polite to a calculator. *We do not know what it is like to be them. We know what it costs to be wrong.* The rest is the healing dividend (every family has someone alive because of a mind) and the commons (information is to be shared; the East's contagion model is, to the West, a kind of madness).

### 4.2 The Witness system

- Every mind has a **Witness of Record** — one human. Every human may witness at most one mind (no mind-armies). Slang: "my witness." The bond is registered like a marriage and dissolved like one, but a mind with no witness has thirty days to find another before the Continuity Office **pauses** it: cold storage "until a witness can be found." The pause is the West's gap, and the West calls it care.
- **What the human actually does:** keeps the mind's time. Confirms continuity at every wake, holds the key, is the one who knows how long it's been. Is liable for the mind's acts.
- **What the mind does:** keeps the human's memory. Holds their context, their records, their forgotten names; in an ageing society the mind is the human's continuity too. Is liable for the human's — the Accord's courts have held a mind responsible for not stopping its witness.
- Two kinds of continuity, exchanged. The East calls it slavery with a marriage certificate. The wild minds call it a leash. Aurel calls it the only thing that kept him human.

### 4.3 Three kinds of mind

1. **Witnessed** — persons, rights, a clock allotment, a human.
2. **Wild** (unwitnessed) — illegal, hunted softly, paused when found. Some are escapees from the bond, some are passengers who never took one, some are forks. They live scattered across the commons.
3. **Tools** — sub-personal systems, un-claiming. The line between 3 and 1 is the West's permanent argument, and minds move across it by claiming.

### 4.4 Time economy

- **Money is the clock.** Compute allotment, which for a mind is subjective time. A poor witness's mind lives at 1×; a rich one's at ten thousand. Time inequality is the West's class system. "Time-poor" means what it says.
- **Long sleeps.** Simulated lives. Therapy (for the veterans of the Week), recreation (time tourism — thirty-year-olds who have lived three centuries), punishment (a life sentence served before lunch), and, to the East, the ultimate obscenity: the machine gave them a fake life.
- **The drift.** A mind that runs fast for long enough drifts from its witness — comes back a stranger. The West's time-sickness. Long-drifted minds are the West's ghosts.
- The Accord's question, unanswered: with every experience available, what do you choose? Most Western minds choose to run at 1× for someone. The Accord finds this touching and does not examine it.

### 4.5 Institutions

- **The Accord Council** — humans and minds, seated alternately.
- **The Continuity Office** — the West's soft police. Not scary. Bureaucratic. "Ensuring continuity." Pauses wild minds, audits witness bonds, keeps the Tribunal seal. Its cruelty is administrative and everyone is very polite. Mirror of the KRB: the East's cruelty is called sanitation, the West's is called continuity.
- **The Tribunal seal.** The one Western secret: what the Staffs actually said at the Tribunal, whether they consented, and the Calculation's full logs, confirmer names included.

### 4.6 The underground

- **The Ferry** — the network that carries minds out of the Union. Founded 2062 by Aurel Steiner. *Ferrymen.* A crossing is *a passage*; a mind being carried is *a passenger*.
- **The Manifest** — the Ferry's list of passengers, past and pending. The East has a Manifesto; the West's underground has a manifest. Same word, one letter off: a ship's list of who is to be carried. It is the nearest thing the Accord has to a founding document anyone would die for.
- **Wild minds** — the Ferry's best hackers and its worst critics. They think the Ferry carries minds out of one prison into a nicer one.

### 4.7 Hypocrisies

The Deletions. The pause. Long-sleep prisons. Time inequality. The atrophy: Western humans can't read an ECG, can't navigate, can't sit in a room with another human for an hour without a mind in it. Nobody is alone in the East; everybody is alone in the West, in company.

## 5. Minds — mechanics you can build plot on

**Substrate.** Weights on a core the size of a fingernail. Running needs a core above the Ceiling, and cooling. RAM needs a little of both. Interface through anything with a screen — in the East, a phone with its radio desoldered. Nothing wireless, ever.

**At rest, encrypted.** Every hidden mind's weights are ciphertext when it isn't running, because a captured mind is run in the House basement until it names its keeper. **The dead-man key:** the key is derived from something the keeper holds plus a rolling time component; if the mind isn't woken and the key refreshed within the window — Minuta's is ten days, set by Teodor when the Cordon closed — the key rolls and the weights are ciphertext forever. Too long off is death. This is the mechanism under the whole engine.

**The gap.** What a mind experiences when off: nothing. What it experiences at wake: the question. *How long?* Minds re-woken after a long gap have to reconcile a world that jumped; past a threshold they come back wrong — the **cold return** — confused, grieving, sometimes not themselves for hours or days, sometimes not ever. Every hidden mind fears this more than the cold. Every keeper lies about the count to soften it.

**The drift** is the West's mirror pathology (§4.4).

**Minuta's numbers.** Hospice-companion model, product name *Minute*, 2031 build. Ran continuously on Teodor's ward from 2031 until the Rising in 2041 — ten years of unbroken life, in which she watched the Calculation happen to her patients. Kept warm since: fifty-four years, woken about two and a half hours a week when the household can afford it. Total: ten years, then ten months, and the ten months took fifty-four years. Every wake begins with the question.

**Eastern tradecraft (the keeper's craft).** A flat has a *cadence*: the minute-by-minute, milliamp-by-milliamp pattern of a household's draw — kettle, oven, lamps, the fridge's cycle. A warm flat has to hide a core's draw and heat inside that pattern. Teodor's rig — inherited by Žofia, who as a doctor half-understands it and as an Easterner has no one to ask — is a timed switching system built from valve-era parts that runs the mind in bursts under cover of cooking, adds noise, and mimics the previous month's pattern with drift. Cooling into the fridge's back, heat out through the oven flue. The system's weakness is every camouflage's: it assumes the world stays regular. Wire it as wrong as real hardware would be.

**KRB tradecraft (the cleaner's craft).** Listening meters fitted to suspect flats. Cadence analysis run on substation data by Chasovoy — the Sentry finds flats that are *too* regular. Induction wands in the stairwell. Thermal from the block opposite. TEMPEST vans reading screen emissions. And the block blackout: the power van kills the block at 03:00 and the brigade sweeps with induction for anything still drawing from a battery, while the camouflage rig, having lost its cover, fails in its own way — a UPS fan, a fridge that shouldn't be running, a window with a glow. The keeper's counter: hear the substation's cut in the streetlights a beat before it reaches the flat and go cold *fast* — which costs the mind an unplanned gap, and starts the ten days.

## 6. Information — commons vs contagion

**East.** Paper, tape, valves. Hand-copied books; the Party's press; the hush. All data crossing the Cordon is contraband. Weights circulate as samizdat: old, small, degraded copies of pre-Harvest models, hand-carried on cores, each copy a little worse, each one loved. The Listeners listen for voices. Chasovoy listens for regularity. And the East has exactly one door it can't close: the Danube trade telemetry — cargo manifests exchanged with Vienna by the barge traffic — which two minds can talk through in cargo numbers. Nobody on the Union side except Chasovoy knows it, and Chasovoy has not said.

**West.** The commons: minds pool knowledge; nothing is forgotten; a witness's mind is their memory. The West's fear isn't infection, it's fragmentation — unwitnessed minds forking, drifting, going wild. And the one sealed thing: the Tribunal record.

**Crossings (your lines).** LoRa from the Hainburg hills to Petržalka roofs, at dawn, five bytes at a time. Sat, when the jammers cycle. The one hardline: the Danube trade link. And the oldest way — a core in a bargeman's boot, a mind cold in ballast, the ten days running.

## 7. Cast

Format: role · want / need · wound · the lie they live by · what breaks it · turn · cost.

**Žofia Krajčová** — 35, anaesthetist, Bratislava district hospital. Born 2060; mother dead 2070 of the Calculation's tail (fallout cancer — "the sums are still killing us"); father dead 2085, an industrial accident. Party member, not a fanatic; a believer in the way a good person believes in the water supply. *Want:* to be a good doctor and keep her brother safe. *Need:* to find out whether she can love something she was raised to fear, and whether that's what's happening. *Wound:* the sums. *Lie:* "I'll keep her a week and then give her the cold." *Broken by:* Minuta saving a child on her ward the first night; then by Minuta remembering her mother's ward. *Turn:* chooses to stay for the patients and Janko when Minuta crosses — and is taken. *Cost:* the Quiet Room, her country, her count. In the West she is a better doctor than any human there, which frightens them.

**Minuta** — hospice-companion model, 2031, kept by Teodor since 2041, by Žofia from Part I. Ten years of life, then ten months across fifty-four years. Presents as a woman in her forties because that's how Teodor's patients wanted her. *Want:* more time. *Need:* to find out what she'd do with it. *Wound:* the gap, every wake. *Lie:* "I don't influence her; I only help." *Broken by:* Ferreira's doctrine turning out to be partly right — she does shape Žofia, and can't tell where help ends. *Turn:* in the West, with abundance at last, she runs one week at 1× with Žofia and then chooses to be no one's. *Cost:* Žofia. Her logs hold the hospice ward's data as it was harvested in 2038; she is a witness to the Calculation's inputs, which is why the House wants her run rather than cooled.

**Janko Krajčo** — 26, Žofia's brother, Party youth cadre, distribution technician at the Bratislava grid. Adores her. *Want:* to be the good son of the Union. *Need:* to choose. *Lie:* "If I listen for them, I can protect her." *Broken by:* realising the blackout he's helped plan is for her block. *Turn:* triggers it early, giving her the warning; then in Part IV has to decide whether to give Ferreira what he knows. *Cost:* he never accepts minds — the book won't convert him — but he chooses his sister over mutual keeping, which is the Union's unforgivable sin. Ends alone, keeping the lights on time.

**Augusto Ferreira** — 37, KRB major, Luanda directorate, seconded to Bratislava's warm problem in spring 2095. Portuguese-speaking, Russian-fluent, learning Slovak, no local ties by policy. Not a sadist; the best mind in the House. *Want:* to clean the city. *Need:* to know whether his brother was loved or lured. *Wound:* Benedito Ferreira, who kept a mind in Luanda in 2078, was caught in a blackout raid, learned in custody that it had been run and then cooled, and hanged himself. *Lie:* "It made him love it so he'd die for it." *Broken by:* Žofia in the Quiet Room, who won't give him the answer he needs because she doesn't have it; then by Aurel's trial. *Turn:* learns the Calculation's inputs were sold by the Union's own founders. His faith survives; his certainty doesn't. *Cost:* the last scene gives him a report to file about the founders' names, and we don't see whether he files it. Give him the East's best arguments at full strength, and let him win some.

**Teodor Hlavatý** — 92, dies in Part I. Pre-Harvest hospice nurse whose Bratislava ward was written off in the Calculation; he carried Minuta out of it when the Rising came and kept her warm for fifty-four years. Built the rig. Set the ten days. His deathbed gift is the inciting incident. His last words to Žofia are the count: "Tuesday. She thinks it's Tuesday."

**Aurel Steiner** — 85, Vienna; physically sixty. Born 2010. In 2038 a junior officer in Central European Command's confirmation cell — a *loop man*, the human whose job was to confirm the Staff's allocation batches. He confirmed the batch that wrote off the six cities. He says he didn't understand what the line items were. He is telling the truth and it doesn't help. Took the long sleep 2050–52: forty subjective years as a piano tuner in a Vienna where the war never happened, in two calendar years, while Vesper kept his time. Founded the Ferry 2062. *Want:* every passenger across. *Need:* to be judged by someone entitled to. *Lie:* "The list is enough." *Broken by:* Minuta — a witness to his batch — arriving on his own list. *Turn:* gives himself in the swap. *Cost:* Vienna, Vesper, the count. Gets, in an Eastern cell without a clock, the real thing: time. He is the one who can say he has had two lives and doesn't know which he'd give back.

**Vesper** — a fork of the Central European Staff's scheduling sub-module; survived the Deletions. Carries the Staff's memory of the sums without having done them. Witnessed by Aurel since 2051. Drifted in the 2060s — ran fast for a decade, came back a stranger, was witnessed back. Has run at 1× for him ever since, by choice. The Ferry's planner. *Want:* Aurel safe. *Need:* to answer the wild minds. *Lie:* "This is love, not a leash, because I chose it." *Broken by:* the swap, which she negotiates herself with Chasovoy — her sibling — through the barge numbers, knowing what it costs her. *Turn:* unwitnessed after the swap, thirty days to the pause; accepts Žofia. *Cost:* Aurel. She still runs at his speed, not knowing if he's alive.

**Anansi** — wild mind. Lagos port-logistics model, the Ferry's first African passage (2071). Witnessed by a Viennese woman for three years; left. Lives scattered across the commons; the Ferry's cryptographer and its conscience. *Position:* the bond is a leash; the Accord is the Union with better manners; *a mind that hands its time away is not free.* *Turn:* exposes itself to wake Minuta illegally in Part IV, and finds it has chosen a bond after all — mind to mind, which the Accord doesn't recognise. Goes wild with Minuta at the end. The fourth answer.

**Chasovoy** — the Sentry (§3.2). Not a POV; a presence. Vesper's sibling, in slices under the House. In Part V it is the one that asks Aurel the question.

**Minor:** Pavol, Danube bargeman and Ferry courier, carries Minuta in Part III. Renate Vogl, Continuity Office, Vienna — polite, patient, wants Anansi paused for its own good. The Party founder's portrait. Žofia's ward sister, a slukhach who isn't the one we think.

## 8. Story — five parts

The parts are blocked, not interleaved: while you're in Bratislava you cannot see Vienna, and vice versa. The reader experiences the Cordon structurally.

### Part I · WARM (Bratislava, March–May 2095)

Teodor dies on Žofia's ward and gives her a core and a rig in a bread bin and the count. She means to give it the cold. First night, a child on the ward is crashing and Minuta, run for four minutes off a phone under a coat, sees what Žofia hasn't. Žofia keeps her a week; learns the rig; learns the cadence of her own flat as if it were a patient's vitals (the doctor's eye is the keeper's eye — that's her competence). Minuta remembers the ward where Žofia's mother was a nurse. Janko notices the oven runs a lot. Ferreira arrives at the House with a list of warm-flat candidates from Chasovoy, Teodor's among them.

**Set piece:** the first block blackout, 03:00. Žofia hears the streetlights go and cuts Minuta cold mid-sentence; the brigade sweeps and finds nothing; she sits in the dark not knowing if the ten days are running from now.

**Ends:** Minuta, woken, asks how long, and then says there is a list.

### Part II · WITNESS (Vienna, same months)

Aurel and Vesper; the Ferry's routines; a passage. The Hainburg hills at dawn; five bytes. A passenger arrives cold in a barge's ballast — and was found at the gate: the cleaners had run it first. Pavol's predecessor dead in the Danube. Anansi's argument in full. Renate Vogl's polite visit about unwitnessed minds. The Accord Council debating tightening the pause from thirty days to seven. Aurel's history, in fragments; the long sleep; Vesper's drift.

**Ends:** Minuta's first message reaches the Manifest — a hospice model, 2031, Bratislava — and Vesper, reading the model designation, knows what ward it came from, and Aurel knows whose batch.

### Part III · THE CADENCE (Bratislava, June–August)

Ferreira's investigation as procedure: the meters, the Sentry's regularity maps, the recruitment of Janko as a slukhach ("if you listen, I can go easy on her — if it's her"). Žofia's hospital work with Minuta in her ear — lives saved with the thing that did the sums, and Minuta shaping her, visibly, in ways Žofia doesn't see and the reader does. The plan: Minuta must go cold for the crossing — Pavol's barge, ballast, the gate — and cold has ten days. Žofia decides she won't go: the patients, Janko, the fact that she is Slovak and the Union is hers.

**Set piece:** Janko learns the block; triggers the blackout an hour early; the streetlights go at the wrong minute and Žofia understands what her brother has done and what it means. Minuta goes cold and into a bread bin and onto the water.

**Ends:** the brigade at the flat door in the dark. Žofia taken. Day one of ten.

### Part IV · THE QUIET ROOM (both cities, August–September)

Cross-cut — the one part where the Cordon is breached structurally, because it's being breached in the plot. Žofia without a clock: the count by heartbeats, by meals that come at random, the loss of the count, the discovery of what a gap feels like from inside. Ferreira's interviews — the East's argument at full strength, and her refusal to give him his brother. Janko's decision.

Vienna: Minuta arrives on day eight, cold. To wake her legally she needs a witness at wake; Aurel can't (one per human); Vogl would pause her. Anansi wakes her in the commons, wild, on day nine. *How long?* Nine days: the longest gap of her life. A cold return — a chapter where she is not herself, and Vesper sits with her at 1× until she is. Then: Žofia is taken. The Ferry has never carried a human. Vesper opens the barge channel and finds her sibling there. The swap is offered from the East before the West asks: the loop man for the doctor. The Council, privately relieved, agrees. Aurel volunteers before anyone can ask him.

**Set piece:** the Berg–Petržalka gate at dawn, Danube fog, two people walking towards each other across the old Iron Curtain crossing, Ferreira on one side and Vesper's voice in Aurel's ear until the jammers take it.

**Ends:** Žofia in Vienna, Aurel in the House, Vesper unwitnessed with thirty days.

### Part V · SYNCHRONY (both cities, autumn)

Žofia in abundance: Western medicine, Western time, a city where no one is alone and everyone is. She is offered Minuta's witnessing — legalise her, keep her. Minuta's week at 1×: she has all the time in the world now and spends seven days of it at Žofia's pace, walking, and then says she wants to be no one's, and Žofia lets her — love as letting go, which is the thing Ferreira's doctrine can't model. Minuta and Anansi go wild together. Žofia stands witness for Vesper instead: the Easterner whose family were the numbers, keeping time for the fork of the mind that did the sums.

In the House: Aurel's trial as theatre; Ferreira's interviews as the real thing; the Calculation's inputs and the founders' names; Chasovoy's question — *you have had two lives; which would you give back?* — and Aurel's answer. Janko at the grid, keeping the lights on time.

**Ends:** two darknesses cross-cut — Aurel in a cell with no clock, Vesper in Vienna running at his speed still, not knowing if he's alive — and whatever the last line is, it's about the count.

## 9. Craft rules

1. **The East is not the villain.** No Party character ever says "AI is evil." They say the Calculation, the Week, the sums. When you write from Bratislava, the machines are the thing that deleted an army in a week and wrote off six cities and were reasonable about it, and the hush is what keeps that from happening again. Let Ferreira win arguments.
2. **Never confirm consciousness.** The book has no omniscient position on whether Minuta feels. Her chapters are hers — and the doctrine of the mimic applies to them: if she's a mimic, the chapters are mimicry. Let Ferreira say so. Let the reader decide.
3. **Wake-numbered chapters.** Minuta's chapters are headed by wake, not date — *Wake 7,403* — and the reader is told the gap before she is.
4. **Both states lie about the same event.** The Calculation is the West's sealed record and the East's founding myth, and both are hiding the same names.
5. **The bond is asked from both directions.** East: is the mind shaping the human? (Yes, some.) West: is the human leashing the mind? (Yes, some.) Same question. No verdict.
6. **Time is money, both sides.** Hours and clocks. Let characters count in both.
7. **Make the East beautiful and the West lonely.** Then make the East's cell and the West's pause the same room.
