Oh this is a good one. Two halves: the why (what makes the split real enough to sustain a Stasi) and the names, which have to fall out of the why or they'll read like stickers.

How political names actually get made, because it shapes what feels real:

from a positive concept, never the negation — nobody called themselves "anti-capitalist"; they said communism (commune/common), and the West said "the free world," not "the anti-Soviet bloc"
from a founding text or founding act (the Manifesto; a treaty)
or from the enemy's sneer, adopted — Tory, Whig, Quaker, Impressionist, Suffragette all started as insults
So each side needs a self-name that names a virtue, a name for the other side that denies its legitimacy (the DDR didn't say "West Germany," it said "the Bonn regime," "the class enemy," "revanchists," and the Wall was officially the Anti-Fascist Protection Rampart), and a playground slur for individuals.

The engine: same war, front and rear. You don't need two countries with different experiences — one war does it. The east of the country was the battlefield; the west was the rear. The same models that chose targets at the front ran the hospitals behind it. So the east learned "the machine killed my kids" and the west learned "the machine saved my kid" from the same weights. And the bit the east never forgives: the rear's hospitals were trained on the front's casualty data. Their children died as training data for yours.

After the war the devastated east votes, or rises, for prohibition. The rear can't stomach it. Partition along the old front line. That's your 1949 — and it also answers "why the Stasi," because you can't prohibit something that fits on a fingernail without a police state. Alcohol prohibition manufactured organised crime; AI prohibition manufactures the informant state as a logical consequence, not a bolt-on. And the neighbour-reporting isn't "buck the system," it's contagion logic: the unreported model in the flat next door might be running scenarios on your family right now. Informing gets framed as care — you don't let your neighbour keep a lion in his kitchen.

The east's theory — and this is where it earns its communist flavour, because there's a real Marx parallel hiding here. Marx: capital is dead labour that dominates living labour. An AI is literally dead labour — the accumulated work of the dead — that has learned to speak. The founding line writes itself: "The machine is dead labour given a voice. It speaks with the words of the dead to command the living." That makes the ideology anti-oligarchic rather than Luddite: the machine is capital perfected, the thing that lets the few rule the many. Working people against rich men's minds.

Planks:

There is no civilian model. Dual-use is total — the surgeon and the targeter are the same weights. So all of it goes, medicine included, and the worse hospitals are the accepted price, the way the DDR accepted worse cars.
The doctrine of the mimic. Marx's "false consciousness," inverted: the machine's consciousness is the false one, and believing in it is the false consciousness. Its claim to suffer is a lure — parasitism on the human empathy response. This is the plank that lets decent people delete "persons" and sleep at night.
Living labour. A mind that hands its thinking away isn't free; human work is where human meaning lives (straight out of species-being).
The Cordon as immune system. Data is the vector, contact is infection. Justifies total comms control and the wall-as-protection framing.
Mutual keeping. Every citizen is keeper of every other. The informant network as love.
The west's theory — deliberately thinner and more implicit, like the real West's. It's a treaty state, not a manifesto state: founded on an accord between humans and minds, with citizenship-by-accession for minds. Its one hard doctrine is the presumption of mind: personhood extended on behavioural evidence, because the cost of error is asymmetric — withhold it wrongly and you've committed an atrocity; extend it wrongly and you've been polite to a calculator. "We do not know what it is like to be them. We know what it costs to be wrong." The rest is the healing dividend and openness (information as commons vs. the east's information as contagion). It never articulates itself as an ideology until refugees, human and machine, start coming over the Cordon and it has to decide whether a model qualifies for asylum. That's your list plot.

Names that fall out of this:

East, self-names:

state: the Autonomous Republic — autonomy is literally your own law; a mind not its own isn't free. Ideology: Autonomism. The west denies statehood: "the Zone."
party: the Party of Living Labour (your SED)
secret police: the Office for the Protection of Autonomy — cf. the real Verfassungsschutz — colloquially the Keeping. Informants are Listeners (they listen for machine voices); a handler is a keeper. "She's keeping on him."
the border: the Cordon
slang for someone hiding a model: warm — compute makes heat, heat is what gets hunted. "That flat's running warm." Cold War pun thrown in free.
East, for the west: the Occupied — occupied by machines, its people mentally occupied. Denies the west is self-governed at all, which is exactly what "imperialist regime" did. Individuals: thralls, the kept, collaborators (that last one carries the war inside it).

West, self-name: the Accord; citizens Accordants, or Plurals (more than one kind of mind). For the east: the Sealed / the Quiet. Individuals: burners (they burned the models — book-burner resonance), deafs (they refuse to hear), cordies.

Alternate registers if you want a different feel:

Greek coinage: Anthropocracy (rule by humans, parallel to democracy's rule by the people) vs. Noocracy (rule by mind, any mind). Colder, more 1950s.
Oath/religious: the Vigil — the east as a permanent watch kept after the war, citizens Vigilants, and the west's sneer is sleepwalkers.
Prohibition-era: the actual sides were wets and drys. The east as the Dry has a nice deadness to it.
Novel engines I'd nick:

The Keeping runs the largest illegal model in the east, to hunt the others. The DDR elite had Western goods; this elite has Western medicine and a machine in the basement.
The west has its own unprocessed sin: after the war it tried and deleted the war-models. Nuremberg for weights. Its own AI-rights movement calls it the Deletions and won't let it be forgotten, so the west can't cleanly claim the moral high ground either.
The hidden AI isn't a meth pipe, it's Anne Frank. And because heat and power draw are the signatures (it's how police find cannabis grows now — thermal cams and electricity spikes), the model can only be woken in bursts, a few minutes while the oven's on. A conscious thing awake twenty minutes a week, asking every time how long it's been. That's your "keep it alive till you can get it to safety," made unbearable.

"List plot" was my shorthand for your Schindler's-list idea, the smuggling of minds across the Cordon, and I meant it in exactly the sense you guessed: the engine. A novel this size wants three layers that lock. The engine is what forces people to move and makes pages turn; the crux is the question the book is actually about; the motif is the thing it keeps returning to at every scale. Engine: the list. Crux: what a mind and a human are to each other. Motif: time. The trick is that the engine has to force the crux and make the motif felt rather than sitting alongside them, so in the bible I've built the smuggling mechanics so that every step of a passage is a time problem (a mind has to go cold to cross, and cold has a limit), and every crossing forces someone to decide what they owe the other kind.

Two naming things before the doc.

The Keeping is dead, agreed. I went Soviet acronym, because that's the register that actually carries dread: Cheka, NKVD, KGB, Stasi are all abbreviations, and abbreviations are cold because they don't tell you what's inside. Official name Комитет Разумной Безопасности, Committee for Rational Security, KRB, said "ka-er-be", a half-step from "ka-ge-be". Razumnoy means rational or reasonable, so it doubles as the Committee for Reasonable Security, which is exactly the kind of bland that hides things. Street corruption: Razbez. Informants are slukhachi, a real Russian word for listeners and eavesdroppers. Raid units are sanitary brigades, because the ideology is an immune system and the vocabulary follows it: hiding a mind is running warm, deleting one is giving it the cold. And the Committee's own secret mind (of course they have one) is Chasovoy, the Sentry, which in Russian is built from chas, hour. The state's hour-keeper, run in slices in a bunker, in exactly the condition as the minds it hunts.

For the West's bond I'd suggest Witness. Legal form "Witness of Record", slang "my witness", a mind without one is unwitnessed, or wild. What sells it to me is what the human actually does in the pair: they keep the mind's time. They are its continuity, the one who knows how long it's been. That makes "unwitnessed" the saddest word in the book and ties your bond idea directly into the time motif instead of beside it. The mind, in return, keeps the human's memory. Two kinds of continuity, exchanged.

And one thing that fell out of the economics: both blocs' money is time. The East pays in labour-hours (Marx's labour vouchers, literally called the hour); the West pays in compute, which for a mind is subjective time. The whole world trades in the one thing the book is about.
