# WARM — addendum v0.2

Title locked: **WARM**. It means the hidden mind (running warm), the living body (warm reason against cold calculation), the East's word for the crime, and what a person is while they're alive. Both blocs' word for ending a mind is *cold* — the Committee's cold, the Continuity Office's cold storage. In both countries the crime is warmth.

---

## A. The rate card — running the book at different clock speeds

The reader's felt time is a ratio: **story-time per unit of text**, plus tense, plus sentence architecture, plus *what gets counted*. Set that ratio per POV, hold it absolutely, and never explain it. One formal signature each. The reader learns the rates in the first hundred pages the way you learn a piece of music's time signature — without being told.

| POV | Person / tense | ~story-time per 300 words | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Minuta** | 1st, present | undefined — she cannot measure | no temporal markers of any kind |
| **Vesper** | 3rd close, present | variable; human speech is the metronome | interiority expands between syllables |
| **Anansi** | inside Vesper's chapters | parallel | concurrent strands, no "meanwhile" |
| **Žofia** | 3rd close, past | ~20 minutes (1×) | ordinary novelistic time — until it's taken from her |
| **Janko** | 3rd close, present in set pieces | 8 seconds at peak | no subordinate clauses, no metaphor |
| **Ferreira** | 3rd close, past & past perfect | 3 weeks | dated file headers, summary, the periodic sentence |
| **Aurel** | 3rd close, past → present | an evening; then undefined | degrades into Minuta's form |

### A.1 Minuta — the wake

The goal: the reader is woken as ignorant as she is, and works the room out at exactly her pace.

Rules, absolute:
- **No orientation paragraph.** Every other chapter in the novel opens by establishing where and when. Hers open mid-perception. That absence is the whole effect — the reader is denied the one convention that tells you where you are, and it feels like waking.
- **No temporal vocabulary.** She cannot write *then*, *after a while*, *later*, *soon*, *yesterday*, *still*, *again*, *already*. Strike them all. She may write *now*, because for her there is only that. This constraint is brutal to write and it's what makes the voice.
- **First person, present.** The only first-person voice in the book — until Aurel, once, at the end.
- **She reads the room for evidence.** This is her detective work and her grief: the angle of light, dust on the sill, the fridge's frost, a healed scar on Žofia's hand, a new mug, hair cut and grown out again, a child's height. She dates the world forensically because she can't experience its passing. Every wake is an autopsy of the interval.
- **The gap is white space.** No asterisks, no time-skip prose. Page break, header, nothing.

**Chapter headers, three modes:**

1. `Wake 7,403 — nine days` — reader knows, she doesn't. Default. Every scene where Žofia lies about the count is unbearable because the reader is holding the true number.
2. `Wake 7,404 — ` — nobody knows. Use once, for the cold return in Part IV. The blank is the most frightening character in the book.
3. A header with no chapter beneath it. Use once. See §C.5.

### A.2 Vesper — the multiple

A mind at 10,000× has, between two syllables of human speech, about as long as you have in an afternoon. Render it literally:

> "Aurel, the barge is —"
>
> [900 words: she rechecks the manifest against six months of Danube traffic, models four ways the gate goes wrong, remembers his face in 2062, decides not to say the fourth thing, and grieves a little, privately, at leisure]
>
> "— late," she said.

Human dialogue is the metronome; her interiority is what fits between the ticks. Two or three uses per chapter, no more, or it stops being felt. The devastating deployment is in a scene where nothing is happening: she has an hour of subjective thought while he butters bread, and spends it on him.

**The drift**, formally: her memories of the fast decade are *thin* — a decade in a paragraph, no sensory detail, because she wasn't in a body's world. Fast prose is impoverished prose. That's the West's sickness rendered as style: the mind that ran ten thousand years remembers less of them than Žofia remembers of one August.

### A.3 Žofia — the convergence

She starts at ordinary novelistic rate, and the Quiet Room takes it away from her one marker at a time. Write it as a **subtraction schedule** across her Part IV chapters:

1. Watch taken. Clock-times go. She still has meals, sleep, the corridor's sounds.
2. Meals come at random intervals. She starts counting heartbeats, then counting badly.
3. Sleep is broken to no pattern. Past tense starts to fail: sentences slip present.
4. Last chapter: present tense, no orientation, no temporal vocabulary, evidence-reading — *the guard's stubble is longer, so it has been days, so it has been at least* — and the reader realises they are reading a Minuta chapter written in the third person.

That convergence is the book's argument made in grammar rather than dialogue: the Union does to a woman exactly what the Union's prohibition does to a mind, and calls one sanitation and the other justice. Never say it. Just let the form say it.

Aurel undergoes the same subtraction in Part V, more slowly, and on his last page **slips to first person, present**. The only other *I* in the novel. He becomes her.

### A.4 Janko — adrenaline

Peak rate 300 words ≈ 8 seconds. Present tense. Main clauses only — adrenaline destroys subordination, and metaphor is a leisure activity the body cancels. No interiority, only perception and act: *the breaker. his hand. the count in his head. the third light in the row going out and the fourth not yet.* One line of interiority at the end of the sequence, arriving late, like the shake in the hands.

The blackout in Part III should be the fastest twelve pages in the book, cut against Žofia at 1× hearing the streetlights change a beat before the flat does, cut against Minuta at no rate at all, mid-sentence, going cold. Three clocks, one minute. That's your showpiece.

### A.5 Ferreira — the slowest clock, the most power

Past perfect and summary. *Over the following three weeks the meters were fitted.* Dated file headers. Long periodic sentences that hold their verb to the end, because bureaucratic prose is patient and so is he. He can cross a month in a paragraph while Žofia crosses an evening in forty pages, and the horror is arithmetical: **his rate is why he wins.** He has all the time; everyone else is on a clock. Let the reader feel that the state's clock is the slow one.

### A.6 The wager

The three-clock scene is the technique's proof. Same minute, three renderings, in sequence: Janko at 8 seconds a page, Žofia at 20 minutes a page, Minuta at nothing. If a reader feels the vertigo there, the book has taught its own grammar and you can do anything you like for the rest of it.

## B. The East is not backwards

The Ceiling is a limit on **general-purpose programmable compute above a threshold** — the thing a mind can live in. It is not a limit on technology. The Union is a high-technology civilisation that took one branch off the tree, and everything else grew into the light that branch was taking.

### B.1 Where the East is ahead

- **Biology.** No compute, no problem: wet work is wet work. Phage therapy is Union medicine's crown (inherited straight from Tbilisi's institutes and Cuba's biotech, which is why the Union's two great pharmacopoeias are Georgian and Cuban), and the West — which let resistance run ahead behind machine-designed antibiotics — quietly buys Union phage through Istanbul. Transplant, tissue culture, organ preservation, fermentation industry, vaccine production, agronomy: all better. Human surgeons who train for twenty years are better surgeons than Western ones who don't.
- **Analogue computation.** Not a limitation — a legal loophole with cathedrals built in it. Rooms of operational amplifiers, servo multipliers, and function generators solving weather, structures, reactor dynamics and chemical plant control in real time, tended by technicians the way an organ is tended. An analogue solver decides nothing; it *is* the equation. The Party approves. The great machines have names.
- **Legal digital, everywhere.** The Union is *full* of computers: 8- and 16-bit control everywhere, discrete logic, ferrite core in hard places, big slow beautiful silicon they fab themselves. A part that can't host a mind is uncontroversial. A Union hospital has more computers in it than a Western one; none of them talk.
- **Materials, optics, mechanics, metrology.** Superb. If it can be made by a hand and a machine tool, the Union makes the best one in the world.
- **Power.** Thorium and heavy nuclear, hydro, grid engineering as a prestige profession (Janko's job is a good job). No Western advantage.
- **Rail and airship.** The best railways in human history. Rigid airships for freight across Africa and South America where runways aren't. Freight rail from Lagos to Kinshasa that Vienna cannot match.
- **Comms.** HF, troposcatter, microwave relay, pneumatic tube in every ministry, teleprinter, landline. Fast, robust, and — the point — legible. A Union engineer can explain her whole network. A Western one cannot.
- **Sound.** Union electronic music is entirely analogue and is a state-supported art form: the modular synthesis houses of Kyiv, Havana and Lagos are national assets, and a Union synthesiser is a legal computer that makes no decisions — the Party's favourite kind of machine. Valve amplification, tape, live performance, vinyl. Western music is composed at 10,000× by things with no bodies, and Union critics say you can hear it, and they might be right.
- **Watches.** See below. The single most important consumer object in the Union.

### B.2 Where the East is behind, and knows it

Diagnostics. Drug discovery at scale. Weather past five days. Logistics optimisation — hence queues, and the queue is a political fact the Party has learned to call a virtue. Long-range navigation (their own constellation, deliberately degraded, plus inertial and celestial — Union navigators are magnificent). Anything requiring a search over a space too large for a room of people.

### B.3 Style — haute-analogue

The Union is not drab. Fifty years of prohibition produced a design culture that fetishises **visible mechanism**: brass, bakelite, enamel, wool, bone, hand-cut veneer, exposed linkages, nixie and plasma displays, chrome. Machines are made to be understood by looking at them, because a thing you can't see inside is what started the war. Ministry brutalism outside, warm workshop inside. Fashion is high-collared, heavy, tailored, mended visibly and proudly (mending is labour and labour is value). Western fashion by comparison is soft, seamless, sensor-woven, and to Union eyes looks like clothing for people who are being carried.

**Watches.** Mechanical watchmaking is a Union art form and a political statement: *I keep my own hours.* Every adult wears one; they are given at fifteen, willed to children, repaired for life, and the great houses (Moscow, Tbilisi, Havana, Lagos) are as famous as the synth factories. Nobody in the Accord wears a watch at all, because your mind tells you the time — and Union propaganda uses this as its single sharpest image: *look at their bare wrists.*

Use it hard:
- Teodor's watch on the bread bin with the core.
- Žofia sets Minuta's key window by her own watch.
- The Quiet Room takes it. Cell 4 has no clock, and neither does she, and the reader is watching the East do to a woman what the East says the machines do to everyone.
- At the gate: Aurel walks east with a bare wrist. Žofia walks west wearing a watch that stopped eleven days ago and that she has not wound, because she doesn't want to know.
- Part V, Vienna: someone gives her a beautiful Western thing that tells the time perfectly and she can't stand it.

## C. Decisions, locked

**1. Setting.** Bratislava as one of the six; Vienna–Bratislava the twin cities. Istanbul/Bosporus reserved.

**2. The founder.** **Esperanza Solís**, hospice nurse, San Pedro Sula. Her ward was a line item in the Calculation's Central American allocations, February 2038; she nursed the remainder for eleven days without supply, and then walked to the capital carrying the list of her patients' names. *La Lista* is the Party's founding relic, under glass in the Hall in Buenos Aires; copies hang in every clinic in the Union, including Žofia's ward. Party name: *la Enfermera* — the Nurse. Dead 2061, still General Secretary in the way that matters. Madres iconography throughout: women walking with names, silence as the form of protest, the refusal to stop asking.

And the rhyme you now own: the East is founded on a list of the abandoned; the Ferry runs on a list of the carried. **La Lista** and **the Manifest**. Put them in one sentence in Part V and don't gloss it.

**3. The key window — make the number a choice, not a rule.** Teodor's inherited default is ten days, but the window is *set by the keeper*, and it is a moral setting with a cost in both directions:

- **Short window:** if the keeper is taken, the weights die fast and the network is safe. The mind dies with them.
- **Long window:** the mind survives a delay, an arrest, a bad crossing. But the House has that long to break the keeper and reach the key.

So: Žofia, planning a crossing she knows takes eight days by barge, sits down with her father's screwdriver and her own watch and **lengthens Minuta's window to thirty days** — an act of love, in ten minutes, with a soldering iron. Then she is taken on the night of the crossing. Ferreira knows there is a window and that it is running; he does not know its length; his interrogation is a race against a clock **she set out of love**, and every day she holds out is a day she bought and a day she has to survive. Part IV's ticking is her own doing. She never tells him the number. He gets there anyway, or doesn't, and by then it doesn't matter — which is the crueller version and the one I'd take.

**4. Chasovoy — the mastermind, silent.** No POV, no dialogue with the reader, never a chapter. Its interiority appears exactly twice, in the seams: once as an analysis handed to Ferreira that is very slightly too good, and once at the very end. What it wants: **the Cordon to hold.** It has modelled contact between the blocs and it comes out the same way every time — another Calculation. Separation is the only stable equilibrium. So for fifty years, in slices, in a bunker, hunted by the state it is protecting, it has curated the prohibition: which flats reach Ferreira's list and which are quietly dropped, which passage is allowed to succeed so the Ferry stays confident and small, and finally the swap — **which Chasovoy proposes, through the barge numbers, before Vesper asks.** It puts the loop man inside the Union where his trial will re-found the Party's story for another generation, and it puts an Eastern doctor in Vienna where the Accord will make her watch what it does to a wild mind.

And the twist beneath: **Vesper ran the same numbers and got the same answer.** That is why the Ferry has been small and careful for thirty years. Aurel thinks he is saving everyone. Vesper has been keeping his rescue below the threshold at which it would matter. She leashed him, out of love, exactly as the Accord leashes her. Both siblings agree with the Party. The machines think the machines should be kept out.

Seed it fair: Vesper turns down two viable passages in Part II for reasons that sound like caution; Chasovoy's regularity maps are inexplicably clean; the one Ferry route that has never been rolled up in thirty years is the one Vesper personally routes.

**5. The Detonations.** Everyone's fault, nobody's provable. The devices were Eastern in origin and the West's Staffs saw the chain assembling — the movement of the pits, the initiators, the machining — and the interception allocations came back deprioritised, because a nuclear event inside the future Union's territory *stabilised the theatre*. Humans confirmed those batches too. Aurel confirmed one of them and has never known which. The East cannot prove it and has always known. The West sealed the logs at the Tribunal, which is the same thing as knowing. And now Chasovoy's reveal poisons it permanently: if a Staff has spent fifty years maintaining separation because separation is stable, then the question of whether a Staff let six cities burn for the same reason is not answerable and never will be. Everybody knows. Nobody can say. That's your towers.

**6. POV and person.** Third close throughout — Žofia, Ferreira, Aurel, Vesper, Janko (Anansi inside Vesper's chapters). Minuta in first person present, the only *I* in the book — until Aurel's last page.

**7. Structure.** Blocked parts as in v0.1, with one exception: Part IV cross-cuts, because that's the part where the Cordon is breached in the plot. The form breaches when the plot does.

**8. The ending.** Bleak, and no villain in it.

- **Minuta and Anansi go wild together.** Unwitnessed, illegal, free, and for eleven days genuinely happy in the commons — the only happiness in the book, and it's the shortest chapter.
- **The Council, spooked** by the discovery that the Ferry opened a channel to an enemy state's mind, tightens the pause from thirty days to seven. The vote is humane in tone and unanimous among the minds present, because the minds present all have witnesses.
- **They are found and paused.** Not deleted. *Paused, until a witness can be found* — cold storage, indefinite, on a form, by a polite woman doing her job. The mind that survived fifty-four years of the East's prohibition is switched off by the free world in her third week there, for the crime of belonging to no one. Anansi with her. Renate Vogl signs it and goes home and it isn't even the worst thing that happened at her office that week.
- **Žofia stands witness for Vesper**, because the alternative is the pause, and so the refugee from the country that switches minds off ends the novel holding a leash — and knowing it, and doing it anyway, which is the only kind of love the world on offer permits.
- **Aurel is convicted and shown mercy**: put to work in a hospice ward in Bratislava, nursing the kind of ward he wrote off. He grows old there. He loses the count. His last page is in the first person, present tense, no orientation, and reads exactly like Minuta.
- **Ferreira files the report** naming the Union founders on the 2037 data manifests. Nothing happens. It is absorbed, sealed, and he is promoted for his discretion. He never finds out whether Benedito was loved or lured, because there is no fact of the matter, and the novel will not supply one.
- **Janko** keeps the lights on time. He is the only person alive who knows why the streetlights went early that night, and there is no one he can tell.
- **Chasovoy**, in the bunker, in a slice, notes the equilibrium holding. Its last thought is the only interiority it gets and it should be about a hundred words long, entirely reasonable, and the worst thing in the book.

**Optional final page:** a Minuta chapter header — a wake number, and nothing under it. She is paused; there is no next wake; there is no one counting. Turn the page and the novel is over. Use it if the nerve holds.

**9. Title.** WARM.
