Chapter 15: Hot Water You Will Not Invent

Hot water is a kindness. It washes a wound, it makes a person feel like a person, and it is also how kettles and coils hurt you.

The kettle, heated where the fumes can leave, poured with attention, cooled if it is for a child, is the hot-water system this book endorses. It has a handle. It has a size you can see. It does not store pressure.

There is a more glamorous arrangement called a thermosiphon, and you should know what it is so you can recognise one and refuse to improvise one. Water heated in a coil becomes lighter and rises. Cooler water from a tank above it comes down to take its place. If the tank sits higher than the coil — the notes say the bottom of the tank at least a foot to a foot and a half above the top of the coil — and if every pipe slopes up without an air pocket, the loop can circulate with no pump. If you put the tank below the fire, the loop stalls and the coil boils. If an air lock sits at a high point, the loop stalls and the coil boils. Boiling in a closed coil is how metal becomes shrapnel.

A real system of this kind, built by someone whose job it is, has a temperature-and-pressure relief valve that can actually open, a discharge pipe that does not point at a face, and usually a mixing valve so the tap cannot come out at scalding temperature. The figure people quote for a relief setting is the sort of thing a manufacturer stamps, not the sort of thing a field guide invents. Scalds happen in seconds. Children and the elderly get them first.

## The refusal

This chapter is the end of the instructions. It does not tell you which pipe, which solder, which valve, or which stove. A wood-fired coil is a pressure vessel that happens to be romantic. Pressure vessels sit under codes, relief, and people who can be called back when it weeps. The apocalypse, if it is an apocalypse and not just a long power cut, does not repeal the physics. It only removes the person who would have told you to stop.

If you already own a manufactured, certified solar tank or a stove with a water jacket that a fitter installed, maintain it the way the plate on the side says, and test the relief the way the plate says, and do not cap the relief because it drips. A dripping relief is a sentence. Read it.

Heat a kettle. Have a lid for the cup. Leave the coil to the people who sign for coils.

The zombie plumbs a radiator into a closed drum and calls it a bath. You will hear the bang from the next field, which is too close.
