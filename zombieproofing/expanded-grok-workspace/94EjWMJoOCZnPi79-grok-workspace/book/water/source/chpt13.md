Chapter 13: Gravity Does Not Send a Bill

A tank uphill of a tap is a pump that does not burn fuel. It burns height, and height is honest.

The rule of thumb in the notes: about 2.31 feet of water column give you one pound per square inch, which is the same statement as about 0.433 psi for every foot of height between the water surface and the tap. A barrel on a stand four feet up is under two psi. That is a polite trickle, enough to fill a kettle without dipping, not enough to be a shower. Ten psi, the sort of sad-but-functional spray people mean when they say "low pressure," wants on the order of twenty-three feet of head. Ordinary house pressure, near thirty psi, wants on the order of seventy feet. You will not get seventy feet by standing on a chair. Do not build a tower you cannot climb down from, and do not climb a tank.

Put the tank where a failure soaks the ground and not the spare room. A full thousand-litre tank is a small car's worth of weight pretending to be furniture. The stand is a structure, not a stack of bricks with optimism. Overflow must have somewhere to go that is not your foundation. The outlet sits a little off the bottom so the sludge can have a neighbourhood of its own, and a valve you can actually close is worth more than a second coat of paint.

## Pipes are also a height, in disguise

Long, skinny, kinked pipe spends the pressure you bought with the hill. Smooth pipe, not tiny, is the habit: the notes mutter about three-quarter-inch to one-inch runs when the distance gets serious. Every elbow is a little tax. A screen on the inlet, a lid on the tank, and no connection that lets garden water or a sprinkler main sip backward into the store. Gravity will also run the wrong way if you give it a path. Backflow is not a moral; it is a missing check valve.

In freezing weather a raised tank is an ice sculpture with a burst in it. Drain what you must, or bury and insulate on purpose, which is a different design and not a jacket tossed over the lid in November.

Gravity will not treat the water. It will only deliver, faithfully, whatever you put in the tank, including the mistake. Treat first, or treat at the tap with a method you can explain. Then enjoy the fact that the hill is working and the generator is not.

The zombie stacks three crates and calls it pressure. You will count the feet, and you will not stand on the lid.
