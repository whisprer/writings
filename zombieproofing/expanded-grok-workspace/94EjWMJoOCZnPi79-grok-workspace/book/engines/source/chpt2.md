Chapter 2: A Map, Not a Licence

This book is a map of the yard. It is not a licence to build the most dangerous object in the scrap pile, and it will not pretend to be one if you read it with sufficient longing.

A map tells you where the ditch is. It does not fill the ditch in, and it does not congratulate you for driving into it at speed because the ditch was clearly labelled. Progress through the tiers in order. The order is the point. The undead, if they are even out there, are not grading you on ambition.

## The six doors, in the only order that counts

1. Observe and identify. Name the machine before you ask it for a favour.
2. Learn the energy flow. Where the heat or the current or the water comes in, and where the waste leaves.
3. Service a complete, known engine. One that already existed, with a maker, a manual, and a limit.
4. Measure before you modify. A guess with a spanner is still a guess.
5. Build small, low-energy demonstrations. Cardboard, code, a hand crank, a certified toy.
6. Only then consider advanced fuels, pressure systems, or high-speed machinery. "Then" may mean "not this year." That is a successful outcome.

## What this pile of pages is actually good at

The draft underneath this voice was strongest on four-stroke petrol engines, the revival of engines that have been asleep, the careful cleaning of a carburettor, the manners of an ethanol conversion, and the reasons biomass gasification can kill you on the way to being useful. Those are the rooms that have furniture.

It is not a manufacturing textbook for making crankshafts, pressure vessels, turbines, or jet engines out of raw ore and a grudge. Where the guide gestures past the materials it actually has, it says so. Context is not a recipe. A paragraph that explains why a scramjet is a bad wheelbarrow motor is not an invitation to build a better wheelbarrow.

## How to raid it

You can read straight through, the way you would walk a yard at first light, or you can open the contents and take the chapter you need the way you would take a filter from a labelled drawer. The first chapters are how not to die of enthusiasm. The middle is what the machine is and what you feed it. The revival sequence is for the day you find an engine that has been plotting. The back of the book is projects you can survive, a diagnostic tree, and a list of things this edition refuses to teach.

Bring a notebook. The notebook will outperform a heroic amount of memory, and it will still be there when you are not. Ink for anything you would hate to lose. Pencil for clearances, pinouts, and opinions.

If a procedure and a law disagree, follow the law. If a procedure and a manufacturer's limit disagree, follow the limit. If a joke and a relief valve disagree, the relief valve has read the incident report, and the joke has not.
