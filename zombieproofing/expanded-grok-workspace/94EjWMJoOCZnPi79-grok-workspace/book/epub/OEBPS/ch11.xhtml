Chapter 11

The Filter in a Pocket

A hollow-fibre filter you can put in a pack is a wonderful thing against the monsters it was built for, and a door left open against the ones it was not.

The fibres people sell for camping often have pores around a tenth to two tenths of a micron. That is small enough to stop protozoa and a great many bacteria, if the filter is intact, if you follow the maker's backflush and lifespan, and if you do not punch a hole in it with a heroic squeeze. Viruses are smaller still, often a few hundredths of a micron. They walk through a fibre that was never asked to stop them. The emergency card is plain about the family of gadgets: most portable filters do not make water safe from viruses by themselves, and even the ones that remove parasites may need a second step. Read the label. "Removes Giardia and Crypto" is a specific boast. "Water filter" is a mood.

So the pocket filter is a clarifier and a parasite gate. The boil, or a chlorine or chlorine-dioxide step done by the label, is what you add when the source might carry viruses, which is any source that has met people or animals. Chemical contamination, again, walks through. A filter stuffed with a little activated carbon will improve taste and can grab some organic chemicals for a while, until the carbon is full, at which point it becomes a small black pebble. Carbon is not a residual. It does not keep working in the bottle overnight, and it does not make a fuel spill into tea.

Care and feeding

Do not let a hollow-fibre freeze if the maker says freezing bursts it. A burst fibre is a short circuit. Do not filter hot water unless the device says it can. Do not assume the squeeze bag's last user had a philosophy of hygiene. Backflush on the schedule, with the cleanest water you have, so you are not packing the dirt deeper and calling it maintenance.

UV pens exist, in the travel kits, and they have their own demands: clear water, the right dose of light, batteries that are not a rumour, and no shady corner of the bottle the light missed. They leave no residual. A dirty finger afterwards undoes them. If you own one, the manual that came with it outranks this sentence.

The zombie taps the filter against a rock "to test it." You will not hear a difference when a fibre snaps. Treat the device as a tool with a life, and treat its output as water that has had one barrier, not as a sacrament.

Pocket first if the water is wild and full of cysts. Kettle or a named disinfectant after, if you cannot swear the source was only pretty. Then the lid.
