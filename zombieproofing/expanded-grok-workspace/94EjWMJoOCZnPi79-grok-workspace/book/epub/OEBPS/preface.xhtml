A note on the drafts

This manual was expanded from a pile: emergency disinfection cards, a rainwater manual, notes on slow sand, a fog-mesh paper, disaster pages about floods and fire and the tap afterwards, and a foundation draft that already knew the unglamorous order of operations. The draft was careful in the way a briefing is careful. What it lacked was the yard. The jokes, the zombies, and the habit of saying a hard rule twice in a human voice were put in afterwards. The public-health spine was not loosened to make room for them.

Where the notes gave a number — a minute of boil, eight drops, a runoff coefficient, a foot of head — the number is still here, and it is still labelled as indicative. Household bleach is not one chemical wearing one hat. Modern bottles wander from about five percent to about nine, and some are not chlorine at all. If the label on your bottle and the table in this book disagree, the label wins, and your local health department wins over both. A historical starting point is not a recipe.

The chapters at the back are refusals as much as they are lessons. The thermosiphon is described so you will recognise one, not so you will plumb one. The well is described so you will wait for a test, not so you will shock-chlorinate a borehole from a humor book. The lightning note is here because a metal roof and a full tank are a serious electrical neighbour, and the neighbour has a code and a profession.

You can read it straight through, or raid the contents the way you would raid a cupboard. The first part is how not to die of enthusiasm. The middle is where water comes from and how you make the biological part of it lonely. The last part is how to keep a cup, how to move it without a bill from the grid, and what this edition will not pretend to teach.

There is a dull warning, and it is dull on purpose. Do not drink flood water to see if you are hardy. Do not use scented bleach because it was nearest the sink. Do not treat a humor book as a boil-water notice. The undead are a metaphor. A cistern of asphalt-roof wash is not.

The dedication, the author's name, and the publisher are blank on purpose. Write them in when you know them. Ink, not pencil. Pencil is for first-flush marks.
