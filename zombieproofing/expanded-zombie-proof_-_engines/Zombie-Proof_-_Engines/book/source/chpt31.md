Chapter 31: Packet Radio and UDP Networking
Packet radio and UDP networking are the backbone of resilient, zombie-proof communication. Dive into the world of packet radio, from building your own transceiver to setting up a network that can withstand the apocalypse. We’ll cover the protocols and hardware needed for reliable communication, and show you how to use UDP for fast, efficient data transfer.

The Basics of Packet Radio
Packet radio uses a variety of technologies to transmit data over radio waves. From Morse code to streaming video, packet radio keeps you connected and informed. In a zombie apocalypse, packet radio is your lifeline to the outside world.

Building Your Own Packet Radio Network
You don’t need a fancy transceiver to get started. With a few scavenged parts, you can build your own packet radio network. Start with a packet radio module, which can be found in old radios, TVs, or even car stereos. Pair it with a microcontroller, and you’ve got the brains of your network. Add a power supply, and you’re ready to roll.

The Protocols of Apocalypse Packet Radio
Packet radio uses a variety of protocols, including AX.25 and UDP. These standards ensure that your network can transmit data from any broadcaster, no matter who built it. In a zombie apocalypse, interoperability is key. You never know what kind of tech you’ll find in a scavenged transceiver, so stick to the standards.

Security in the Apocalypse
Security is a major concern when the world is falling apart. Packet radio uses encryption and authentication to protect your data. In a zombie apocalypse, you’ll want to use the strongest encryption available. Look for systems that support AES-128 or higher, and always keep your software up to date. If you’re building your own system, make sure to implement secure key management and regular firmware updates.

The Future of Apocalypse Packet Radio
As the world rebuilds, packet radio will become even more important. With the rise of digital broadcasting and decentralized communication, you might find yourself building your own network from scratch. Whether you’re using AX.25, UDP, or something entirely new, the future of apocalypse packet radio is bright.
