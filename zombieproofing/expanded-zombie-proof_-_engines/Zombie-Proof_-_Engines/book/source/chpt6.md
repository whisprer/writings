# Chapter 6: Projects and Practical Builds — Fox Hunting, Reverse Engineering & Survivor Hacks

## Introduction: Time to Get Your Hands Dirty

Theory is cool and all, but the apocalypse doesn’t care unless you turn knowledge into homebrew radios, tracking gear, or hacked comms. This chapter loads your toolkit with easy-to-follow, zombie-tested projects from hunting hidden signals to reverse engineering random remotes.

---

## Project 1: Radio Fox Hunting — Tracking Signals Like a Zombie Pro

- Purpose: Find hidden transmitters by triangulating their signal direction.
- Gear: Simple directional antenna, receiver, compass, notebook.
- Method: Walk and rotate, note signal strength and direction at various points.
- Use triangulation math to zero in on signal origin.

---

## Project 2: Reverse Engineering a Remote Control

- Salvage a broken remote from the rubble.
- Tools: Basic radio frequency receiver/debugger or SDR.
- Decode transmitted codes by capturing signal patterns.
- Replay hacks: replay signals with transmitter or SDR to control remote devices.

---

## Project 3: Build Your Field Strength Meter

- Parts: Wire loop antenna, diode (1N34A germanium recommended), analog meter.
- Construction: Assemble as schematic to measure signal strength visually.
- Use: Locate transmitter proximity or check antenna performance instantly.

---

## Project 4: DIY Transmitters and Signal Boosters

- Build a low-power AM or FM transmitter from scavenged electronics.
- Use variable capacitors and inductors to tune frequency.
- Safety: Always comply with legal power limits (a max 1500W transmitter project discussed later).
- Tip: Pair transmitter with gain antennas to maximize range.

---

## Project 5: Software Defined Radio (SDR) Basics

- Use cheap SDR USB sticks and open-source software.
- Receive signals from a massive range of spectrum bands.
- Experiment with decoding paging systems, weather satellites, or emergency broadcasts.
- Write Python scripts for automation and signal decoding.

---

## Practical Survival Tips

- Always carry a fiber or copper wire for impromptu antennas.
- Have multiple hand-held scanners tuned to different bands.
- Learn to build and test your gear from scratch to avoid zombie tech strikes.

---

## Memory Aid: Quick Build Checklist

- Antenna type and length ready.
- Power source (battery, solar).
- Receiver/transmitter assembled.
- Test signal strength and range.
- Log frequencies and codes.
