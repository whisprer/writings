Chapter 36: Protocols and Security in the Apocalypse
In the zombie apocalypse, communication isn’t just about sending a message—it’s about making sure that message stays secret, secure, and doesn’t get hijacked by the undead or rival survivor gangs. Radio protocols and security are your digital armor, shielding your transmissions from prying ears and malicious interference.

The Protocol Zoo
Radio protocols are the rulebooks that govern how your signals behave. From simple Morse code to complex digital packet systems, each protocol has its strengths and weaknesses. In the apocalypse, you’ll want protocols that are robust, easy to implement, and resistant to interference.

Morse Code: The granddaddy of all protocols, Morse code is simple, reliable, and can be sent with minimal equipment. It’s also hard to jam or intercept, making it perfect for emergency signals.

AX.25: This packet radio protocol is the backbone of amateur digital radio. It’s designed for noisy, unreliable channels and includes error checking and addressing, making it ideal for sending text messages or data over long distances.

UDP: For fast, connectionless communication, UDP is your go-to. It’s perfect for short bursts of data, like alerts or status updates, but lacks the reliability of more complex protocols.

Security: Keeping the Zombies Out
Security in the apocalypse isn’t just about encryption—it’s about making your communications as hard to intercept or disrupt as possible.

Encryption: Use simple ciphers or one-time pads to scramble your messages. Even basic encryption can keep your communications safe from casual eavesdroppers.

Rolling Codes: For repeated transmissions, use rolling codes that change with each message. This prevents replay attacks, where an attacker records your signal and plays it back later.

Frequency Hopping: Switch frequencies regularly to avoid jamming and eavesdropping. This technique is used in Bluetooth and some military radios, and it’s surprisingly effective against both zombies and hackers.

Practical Tips
Always use strong, unique passwords for your radios and networks.

Regularly update your protocols and security measures to stay ahead of new threats.

Keep your communications brief and to the point to minimize the risk of interception.

With the right protocols and security measures, you can keep your messages safe and your survival network thriving, even in the face of zombie hordes and digital threats.
