Afterword

The last chapter of an engine book is a temptation to get solemn about civilisation. Resist it a little. The yard is not a chapel. It is a patch of ground you share with whoever needs water moved, grain milled, or a battery persuaded back to life, and the courtesy that keeps it usable is older than any of us: check the oil, point the exhaust away from the living, and leave a way to stop the machine that does not involve a hero.

If you have read this far you already know the unglamorous truth. Torque without control is a rumor. Power without cooling is a fire. The question here is not how loud the engine can be. It is whether the next person can start it, load it, and shut it down without becoming the incident. The person who brings a spare filter will be remembered more kindly than the person who brings a speech.

The zombies, if you still need them, are only the name we gave the interruption. A dry start is one. A belt with no guard is one. Panic that wires the choke open and walks away is one. You do not beat them by adding boost. You beat them by being able to explain, on paper, what the machine is supposed to do when the morning comes back.

Leave a note on the cowling. Tell someone else the oil grade, the fuel, and where the stop is. When you are done for the night, say so, and shut it down on purpose, so nobody sits up listening to an idle out of loyalty.

The signature under an afterword belongs to the person who stands by the book. That line is blank until you want it.

Check the oil. Then quiet.
