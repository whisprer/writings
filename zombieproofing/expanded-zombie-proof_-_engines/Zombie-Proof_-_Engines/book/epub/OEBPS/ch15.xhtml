Chapter 15

The Advanced Gallery, Told Without Romance

This is a gallery. The objects are on the shelf so you can name them. They are not a shopping list, and they are not a weekend. The neighbour reaching for the turbo can keep reaching. The hand in the plate is there to be a no.

Diesel

A diesel squeezes air until the air is hot enough to light the fuel as the fuel is injected. There is no premixed spark-lit charge in the basic cycle. The compression is high. The injection pressure is high. The pistons, rods, bearings, and the cleanliness of the fuel system all have to be up to a standard that does not forgive a rusty drum. Diesels tend to be heavier and slower than petrol engines. They pay you back with low-speed torque and a better habit of turning fuel into work when the load stays steady. They are excellent servants and bad improvisations.

Atkinson, Miller, and the hybrid's bargain

Valve timing can make the effective compression stroke shorter than the expansion stroke. You give away some specific power and you gain efficiency. Hybrids like this, because an electric motor can cover the moments when you want to be quick and the piston engine would rather be wise. It is a timing strategy, not a new element.

Boost, which is a loan

A turbocharger spends exhaust energy on a compressor. A supercharger is driven by the engine directly, so it takes its pay before the exhaust has anything to say. An intercooler cools the squeezed charge so it gets denser. More oxygen lets you burn more fuel. It also raises pressure, heat, the risk of knock, and the load on every part that was sized for a quieter life. Boost is not free horsepower. It is a loan from the parts department, and the parts department charges interest in pistons.

Valves that change their mind

Variable cam timing moves the moment the valves open and close, to buy low-speed torque or high-speed breathing or a cleaner burn. Variable lift goes further. Get the timing wrong on an interference engine and the piston meets the valve. The meeting is brief. The bill is not.

The rotary

A roughly triangular rotor turns eccentrically in a housing shaped for the job. Compact, smooth, and utterly dependent on its apex seals and its side seals. The chamber is an odd shape. The heat and the emissions are not rumours. Admire it. Do not assume the scrapyard owes you a seal kit.

Turbines, and the machines that only work when they are already flying

intake → compressor, or compression by sheer speed → burner
→ turbine and nozzle → a shaft, or thrust

A turbojet makes thrust by throwing mass backwards. A turboprop takes much of that energy and gives it to a propeller. A ramjet has no compressor; the speed of going forward does the squeezing, which means it is useless until it is already very fast. A scramjet keeps the flow inside supersonic, and lives only in a specialised regime you will not be visiting with hand tools. These are not shed projects. The rotating parts, the hot section, the fuel system, the noise, and the energy in a failure are all severe.

Where turbine engines are actually operated, they are kept inside limits of temperature, rotational speed, and — where it applies — torque. The limit is the machine. Your enthusiasm is not.
