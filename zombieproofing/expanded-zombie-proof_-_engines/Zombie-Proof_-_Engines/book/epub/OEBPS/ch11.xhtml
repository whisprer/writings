Chapter 11

Bath, Spark, and the First Honest Start

There are three things a spark engine needs before it will consent to run: a mixture it can burn, compression enough to make the burn worth having, and a spark at the right moment. Everything else is costume. The costume can still kill you, which is why the costume gets a procedure.

mixture + compression + a spark at the right time

If it cranks and does not catch, you are not cursed. You are missing one of the three, or you are flooded, blocked at the exhaust, or being overruled by a safety interlock that is, for once, the adult in the room.

The carburettor, washed rather than attacked

The cleaning method in the source material is a low-caustic hot-water bath for parts that can stand it, taken fully apart:

  • Photograph the settings. Count the turns of the mixture screws before they come out. Write the count where yesterday-you cannot lose it.
  • Remove jets, float, needle, gaskets, and anything that should not be cooked. Linkages too, if they are not the thing you are cleaning.
  • A bath around ninety-three degrees Celsius, two hundred Fahrenheit, watched with a thermostat. Not a rolling boil you wandered away from.
  • Something like twenty to thirty minutes in a mild detergent or degreaser.
  • A soft brush. Soft nylon to clear the calibrated holes. Clean compressed air to dry them.
  • Do not "clean" a jet with a wire that is secretly a file. If you enlarge a calibrated hole you have changed the fuelling, and you have done it in the dark.
  • Replace seals and gaskets that have given up.

This removes deposits. It does not necessarily remove the stain on the aluminium, and the stain is not the problem. The problem is the passage you cannot see and therefore decided was probably fine.

The spark, tested like an adult

Battery state. Clean terminals. Whether the starter actually engages, and what it draws. Coil resistance against the specification, not against a feeling. Plug condition and gap. The leads: continuous, and insulated. Distributor, rotor, cap, points, or the electronic trigger, whichever century you are in. Ground paths that are actually ground.

Test a spark away from fuel vapour, with the proper tester and the proper procedure. Do not hold a plug lead in your hand to see if the night still has electricity. That is not diagnosis. That is volunteering for a reboot of the nervous system, and the engine will not even thank you.

Air, compression, timing

Look at intake hoses, manifold gaskets, the PCV path, and the throttle body for leaks. A proper smoke test beats spraying something flammable around a hot engine, which is a method favoured by people who like stories more than eyebrows. A compression test, if the engine is fit to be tested, tells you whether the rings, the valves, and the gasket are still a team.

Line the crankshaft and camshaft marks up at the correct number-one top dead centre. Interference engines bend valves when the timing is a fairy tale. Non-interference engines merely run like a sack of spanners. Neither is a personality you want.

The first start, which is a ceremony of suspicion

Outdoors. Clear of people, fuel stores, and anything that burns if a backfire gets ideas. Fresh fuel of the correct kind. The moment it catches, confirm oil pressure. Then look, in this order, for leaks, a noise that is new, a runaway, smoke, heat, and where the exhaust is actually going.

Blue smoke suggests oil in the burn. White smoke can be coolant, or only the condensation of a cold start; if the white stays, it is no longer a mood, it is a diagnosis. Black smoke often means too much fuel, but the engine type and the moment matter. Do not hang a load on an engine that has only just remembered it is an engine. If you expected the oil to pick up years of debris, change the oil and the filter again after the first careful run.

For an engine that has slept for decades, the source protocol would rather you take it apart than trust a key. A key is not a resurrection. It is a question. The answer, if you have done the left-hand end of the list, might be a machine that idles in the open air while you watch the gauge and do not narrate.

The neighbours in the plate have their ear defenders on sideways. You will wear yours the right way round. The exhaust points at the weather, not at the house.
