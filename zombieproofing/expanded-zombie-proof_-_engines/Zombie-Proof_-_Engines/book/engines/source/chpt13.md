Chapter 13: Steam You Will Not Invent

Steam is external combustion with a long commute.

```text
fuel + air → furnace → water becomes steam under pressure
→ piston or turbine → condenser or exhaust → feedwater back
```

The romance is real and old. The hazard is the boiler, and the boiler is not a beginner project. Homemade pressure vessels are how intelligent people become a cautionary paragraph. The guidance on pressure systems — suitable construction, devices that limit pressure, a gauge you can read, a relief valve that is not a suggestion, inspection, and operation by someone competent — exists because steam stores energy in a way that does not care about your narrative.

For learning, use one of these, and not a clever fifth:

- A low-pressure educational model that was designed and certified for the job.
- An open, unpressurised demonstration: sun on a pipe, water getting warm, nothing trying to be a bomb.
- A commercially made boiler with written limits, used inside those limits.
- A calculation of a Rankine cycle on paper, which has never scalded anyone.

The plate shows the certified toy, and the ordinary cooker left on the shelf. The neighbour is disappointed. Disappointment is survivable. A substituted pressure vessel is not. "It held water yesterday" is not a test. Water is heavier when it is steam only in the sense that the room becomes full of a fact you cannot put back.

## The spark, which is a chain and not a dare

A basic ignition path:

```text
battery or alternator → switch → coil primary
→ the field collapses → high voltage on the secondary
→ the plug gap → spark → the burn, if the mixture deserves it
```

An alternator is an accessory with a degree. Rotation changes the magnetic flux and a current appears. It needs regulation, or it will overcharge the battery and cook whatever you hung on the line. A safe way to learn the chain is a current-limited bench supply, an ignition module meant for teaching, and a commercial spark tester. Do not build a high-voltage circuit on a breadboard beside a fuel can. The breadboard is not the hero of this story.

## The motor that does not burn

An electric motor is a generator persuaded to run backwards. Current in the windings makes fields; the fields argue with magnets or with another winding; the argument is torque. Speed depends on voltage, load, the windings, the controller, and the back-voltage the motor itself produces as it spins. A battery drivetrain is usually:

```text
battery → fuse and contactors → controller → motor → a reduction gear
         ↘ a battery-management system watching ↗
```

It can be efficient, quiet, and precise, which makes it feel harmless. Batteries are not harmless. They want a charger that knows them, a temperature they can live in, isolation you can trust, and a fire plan that was written before the smoke. A quiet machine can still be the thing that burns the workshop down at three in the morning, which is a poor time to invent a plan.

Use electricity where you have the wire or the cells, and where the load likes a motor more than it likes a flywheel. Use combustion where you have the fuel and the exhaust path and the maintenance habit. Do not use steam as a personality. The settlement needs torque, not a legend, and the legend is how the relief valve gets tied down "just for the afternoon."
