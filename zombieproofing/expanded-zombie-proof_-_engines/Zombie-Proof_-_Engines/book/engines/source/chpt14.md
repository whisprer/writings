Chapter 14: From the Crank to the Wheel

The engine rarely shakes hands with the load directly. There is a etiquette in between, and the etiquette is a set of parts that fail in boring, predictable, preventable ways.

```text
engine → clutch or coupling → gearbox or reduction
→ differential or final drive → axle and wheel
```

- **A gearbox** trades speed for torque. Low gear multiplies the twist and slows the shaft. High gear does the opposite. Neither gear creates energy. They spend it in a different costume.
- **A clutch** lets the engine live while the load sits still, and lets the engagement be a slope instead of a cliff.
- **A differential** lets the driven wheels turn at different speeds in a corner, which is the difference between a vehicle and a plough that has become confused.
- **A belt** is cheap and a little forgiving, and it will slip when it should, and it will also take a finger if you give it one. Guard it.
- **A chain** does not slip if it is honest, and it wants oil and alignment, and it will also take a finger. Guard it.
- **Direct drive** is efficient and has fewer parts, and it demands that the engine's happy speed and the load's happy speed are already friends.
- **A generator coupling** has to keep the engine at the speed the alternator was promised, with a governor that means it, and with protection against the day the load disappears and the speed does not.

The plate puts a guard over the belt before the neighbour's curiosity arrives. Curiosity is not a tooling allowance. Pinch points do not negotiate, and they do not prefer zombies. If you are building the teaching version, Chapter 16 will insist on a hand crank or a low-voltage motor, a guard, and no glory.

## Budget the journey, not the noise

On a road or a rail, the energy has to pay for rolling resistance, the air, the hills, the cargo, the desire to speed up, and the weather, which is a tax. A slow diesel may be glorious at pulling a heavy train and hopeless at making a light vehicle feel alive. A fast petrol engine may be the other way round. Match the prime mover to the job, then match the gearing to the match. A brilliant engine in the wrong ratio is a brilliant way to overheat at walking pace.

For a pump or a mill, write down the speed the driven machine wants, the speed the engine is willing to hold, and the ratio that joins them. Then put a guard on it, a way to stop it, and a person who knows which is which. The person is part of the transmission. When the person leaves, the note on the cowling has to be able to do their job for the first five minutes. That is all a note can do. It is enough to stop the next person joining the belt.
