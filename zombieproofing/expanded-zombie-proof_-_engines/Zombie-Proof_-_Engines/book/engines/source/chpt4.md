Chapter 4: Four Strokes and a Flywheel

If you learn one picture, learn this one. It is not sacred. It is just the machine you will actually find, drawn without its shyness.

```text
                 cylinder head
          intake valve   exhaust valve
               ↓              ↑
        ┌─────────────────────────┐
        │     combustion space    │
        │       ┌────────┐        │
        │       │ piston │        │
        │       └───┬────┘        │
        └───────────┼─────────────┘
                    │ connecting rod
                    │
              ──────┴────── crankpin
             /              \
        main journal      crank throw
                    crankshaft
```

The cylinder is a cup that must hold pressure and guide a sliding plug. The piston is that plug. The rings on the piston try to keep the fire above and the oil below, and to hand heat sideways into the wall. The connecting rod is the diplomat: the piston wants to go up and down, the crankshaft wants to go round, and the rod attends the negotiation. The crankshaft turns the negotiation into rotation. The flywheel remembers the one stroke that made power, and spends that memory on the three strokes that did not. Without it, a single-cylinder engine is a series of arguments separated by silence.

## The cycle, which takes its time

| Stroke | Piston | Valves | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intake | Top to bottom | Intake open | Air, or air and fuel, comes in |
| Compression | Bottom to top | Both closed | The charge gets smaller, hotter, and less patient |
| Power | Top to bottom | Both closed | Spark or the heat of compression releases the energy; the gas expands and pushes |
| Exhaust | Bottom to top | Exhaust open | The spent gas leaves, ideally outdoors |

One complete four-stroke cycle takes two revolutions of the crankshaft, seven hundred and twenty degrees. Only the power stroke pays the rent. The flywheel, and any other cylinders you were lucky enough to find, carry the engine through the rest. The camshaft opens the valves at the right crank angle. In an ordinary four-stroke the cam turns at half the crankshaft speed, which is why a timing belt is not a fashion accessory. Get the relationship wrong on an interference engine and the piston and the valve try to occupy the same room. They cannot. The valve loses.

## What the rest of the cast is for

- **Spark plug:** a timed high-voltage arc, in engines that ask for one. Not a thing you hold in your hand to "see if it sparks."
- **Injector or carburettor:** the measuring cup. It does not have a sense of humour about being enlarged with a drill.
- **Oil pump and sump:** friction's enemy, and the postman for heat and tiny pieces of metal you would rather know about.
- **Cooling system:** moves heat from the metal into air or coolant and then into the weather. If it stops, the engine starts spending itself.

Top dead centre is the piston's highest point. Bottom dead centre is its lowest. You will see them written as TDC and BDC in every manual that has met a mechanic. Say them out loud once so they stop looking like a code.
