Chapter 3: What an Engine Is For

An engine converts an energy source into useful motion. The source may be chemical fuel, steam pressure, compressed air, electricity, falling water, or — in machines you will not be building behind the shed — nuclear heat. The motion may be rotation, reciprocation, or thrust. That is the whole family secret. Everything else is manners, metallurgy, and not setting the yard on fire.

A petrol engine burns its fuel inside the cylinder. A steam engine burns fuel in a separate furnace and uses the heat to make steam, which then does the pushing. A jet engine accelerates air and combustion products rearwards and calls the recoil thrust. An electric motor skips the fire and turns electromagnetic force directly into rotation, which is why it can be quiet enough to make a zombie feel judged.

## The pattern, which does not care about your costume

```text
energy source → working fluid or electromagnetic field → force → motion → useful load
```

Read it until it is boring. Boring is how you notice which arrow you skipped. A machine is not useful merely because it runs. A machine that runs and cannot reject its waste heat is a kettle with ambitions. A machine that runs and cannot survive its load is a noise. A machine that runs and cannot be controlled is the metal grenade from the first chapter, now with an audience.

Useful means four things at once:

- It is safe enough that the operator is still the operator afterwards.
- It rejects waste heat on purpose, into air or water or exhaust pointed at nobody you like.
- It survives the load it was asked to carry, including the dull loads: idle, heat, dust, a long hill.
- It delivers power through an interface you can actually govern. A clutch, a valve, a switch, a stop that works when your hands are cold.

## What you will mostly meet

Most of the engines in a fallen parts bin are four-stroke piston engines wearing different coats: a generator, a walk-behind tractor, a pump, a motorbike that has taken the news badly. Learn that creature properly and the others become variations, not religions. The coat changes. The manners — oil, cooling, a timed breath, a way to stop — do not.

There is a snobbery that says a real engine must burn something and make a satisfying sound. Ignore it. The settlement that can pump water with a quiet motor and a repaired wire is ahead of the settlement that has a magnificent knock and a funeral. Use the machine that matches the fuel you can actually keep, the load you actually have, and the person who will have to fix it on a wet Tuesday.

A car engine is not automatically a good generator engine. A jet engine is an exceptionally bad wheelbarrow motor, unless the wheelbarrow is trying to leave the planet, in which case you have a different book and a much shorter future.

The rest of this part takes the four-stroke apart slowly, then lines the family up so you can see which cousins are worth inviting to the yard and which ones should remain stories.
