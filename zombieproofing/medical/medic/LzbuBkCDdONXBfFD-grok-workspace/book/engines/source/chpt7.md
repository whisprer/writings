Chapter 7: Fuel, Vapour, and the Travelling Puddle

Fuel is not a sauce. You do not cheer it up. You store it, you meter it, and you keep it away from anything that thinks it is a spark. The puddle in the plate is already leaving. Petrol vapour does not sit on the liquid like a polite fog. It travels, along the floor, toward the lowest stupid decision in the room.

| Fuel | What it offers | What it demands |
|---|---|---|
| Petrol | Volatile, dense enough with energy, happy to be sparked | Fire from the vapour, short storage life, knock if you get proud |
| Diesel | Dense, not eager to vapourise, lit by hot air | High injection pressure, a clean system, a cold-start plan |
| Ethanol | High knock resistance, can be made where plants still grow | Less energy per litre, drinks water, attacks some materials, sulks when cold |
| Biodiesel | Plant oils or waste oils, after they have been properly made into fuel | Thickness, oxidation, cold flow, deposits, hoses that disagree |
| Wood gas | Biomass, if you accept a poorer and more poisonous breath | Carbon monoxide, tar, ash, water, less power, a real chance of explosion |
| Hydrogen | Burns over a very wide mix, and quickly | Leaks, embrittlement, eagerness to light, storage that is a project of its own |
| Coal or wood | Heat you can often still find | Ash, smoke, tar, a boiler's bulk, and no interest in being metered neatly |

Changing fuel is never "just pour it in." The new fuel changes the mixture, the timing, the temperatures, the seals, the filters, the way you store it, and sometimes the compression ratio the engine was willing to live with. A machine that was honest on its old diet can become a liar on the new one, and liars in this yard knock, overheat, or melt something small and expensive.

## Storage, which is a character test

The notes under this book give petrol a rough shelf life of three to six months before varnish and oxidation become the main plot. Heat, air, a dirty can, and ethanol blends that have found some water will shorten that. Old fuel is not a challenge. It is a varnish factory. Drain it. Label the can. Do not "see if it still goes" inside a building because the rain has started.

Keep approved containers, outdoors or in a lawful store, away from the living and away from anything that sparks. A generator belongs away from windows, doors, and vents. Exhaust that can be sucked back indoors is exhaust you aimed at your own lungs. An inversion, one of those still evenings when the air refuses to rise, will hold that exhaust near the ground like a blanket. A quiet engine is not a safe engine. Carbon monoxide has no interest in the view.

If you are converting rather than feeding, read the next two chapters before you touch a jet or light a drum. One of them is about measurement. The other is about not dying in a shed that smelled, briefly, of woodsmoke and good intentions.
