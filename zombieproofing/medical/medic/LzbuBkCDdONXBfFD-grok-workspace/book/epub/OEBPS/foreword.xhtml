Foreword

Someone always asks, at the bad moment, what to do. They mean the blood. They mean the person who has gone quiet. They mean the cupboard, which is suddenly more interesting than it was on Tuesday.

This manual is an answer with a limit on it. It begins with the scene, because a clever dressing in a room full of smoke is a second patient. Then it says the civilian order: help, breathing, bleeding you can see, warmth, and a note the next person can read. It will not turn you into a surgeon because the phone is sulking.

You will find wounds, bites, bones, burns, and the long dull middle in which people either get better or do not. You will find a chemist’s list that is mostly soap, and a storage chapter that refuses to bury a barrel of mixed tablets and call it a plan. Later chapters are nursing, the people who are not a standard adult, and the mind that has to stay in the room. The last chapter is cards, for the hour your excellent brain has left.

The zombies are a convenience. They stand in for hurry, folklore, and the part of a person that would rather hold a crystal lamp than a bar of soap. If you are reading this while the world is, against the odds, still selling dressings, the zombies will not mind. The body is the same on a Tuesday.

A word about the pages that sound like a hospital. Curiosity is not a licence. Do not cut a neck because a footnote admitted that trained people sometimes do. Do not loosen a tourniquet because a folder in the research pile had a timetable. Do not break into a clinic because a sentence explained why the locked cupboard is not yours. The jokes are jokes. A failed phone call does not confer a qualification.

What I hope you keep is not a gadget. It is the habit of a person you can hand over: what happened, what you did, what changed, and where the torch is. Medicine is poor company and an excellent servant. It does not replace the person who learned it. It only makes it possible for the morning to include the living.

Leave your name off the title page until you want it there. The kit does not require a byline. It requires soap, a note, and somebody who comes back to check.
