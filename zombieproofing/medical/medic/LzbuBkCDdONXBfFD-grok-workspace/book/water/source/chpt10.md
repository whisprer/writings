Chapter 10: Slow Sand and the Schmutzdecke

There is a filter that is mostly sand, gravity, and a living skin, and it has been making water kinder for a long time. The grand version is a slow sand bed at a treatment works. The household cousin people mean, when they are off the grid, is a biosand filter: a box with gravel at the bottom, coarser sand in the middle, fine sand on top, and a flow so slow it feels like a philosophy.

Four things happen at once, if the filter has been built to a real design and has been allowed to grow up. The sand strains out particles and many of the larger cysts. Grains, and the iron films on them, catch microbes by stickiness as much as by size. In the top centimetre or two a biological carpet forms — algae, bacteria, protozoa — which the trade calls the schmutzdecke, a word that sounds like a roommate because it is one. That layer eats and competes. Deeper down, in poorer oxygen, a lot of what was trapped simply fails to thrive. A mature, slow, properly minded filter can take out the great majority of protozoa and a large share of bacteria and viruses. The figures in the notes — better than 99.9 percent of the protozoa, and often 90 to 99 percent of the bacteria and viruses — belong to filters that are actually working, not to a bucket of beach sand with a tap.

## It is not a blessing and it is not finished

A new biosand filter is a pile of geology. The schmutzdecke takes time, on the order of weeks, and it wants to stay wet. If you let the sand dry, you kill the roommate and you are back to straining. If you pour water through as fast as a tap, you are not doing slow sand. You are rinsing. Designs from the organisations that publish them — bed depths, grain sizes, a standing head of water, a flow you can count in litres per hour and not in excitement — are the designs. This chapter will not invent a new one in a humorous voice. A humorous sand filter is how people get confident and sick.

Even a good one is a barrier, not the whole fence. The public-health habit is multi-barrier: settle, then a real filter, then boil or a chlorine dose you can name, especially for viruses, which are small and rude. Sand does not remove dissolved metals or fuel. If the water went in smelling of a solvent, it will come out with a cleaner complexion and the same bad character.

Maintenance is mostly restraint. You do not dig the sand out because it looks mucky on top; that muck is the job. When the flow slows too far, the published designs tell you how to stir and discard only the very top. A filter with a cracked body, a dried bed, or a bypass hose "for when we are in a hurry" is a bypass.

Name the roommate if you want. Do not ask it for a certificate. After the sand, the kettle still gets an introduction.
