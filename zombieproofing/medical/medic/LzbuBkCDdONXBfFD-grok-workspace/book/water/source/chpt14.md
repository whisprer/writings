Chapter 14: The Buried Pot

A garden drinks like a person who has never heard of a bill. Spray it at noon in a wind and you can lose half or more of the water to air and to the leaves' vanity. The old fix is a pot.

An olla is unglazed clay, the porous kind, buried to its neck among the plants and filled with water, then covered so the neck is not a mosquito's ballroom or a kitten's tragedy. Dry soil pulls water through the clay. Wet soil, after rain, stops pulling. The roots come to the damp and stay there, which means less weed festival on the surface and less evaporation. Gardeners who use them at a sensible spacing talk about cutting a large share of the water they used to throw at the foliage — the enthusiastic notes say up to about seventy percent against wasteful surface watering. That is a garden figure, not a promise about your sand, your wind, or your tomatoes' emotional needs.

The shape you want has a belly and a neck you can find. A glazed pot does not weep; it is a jar, and a jar buried full of water is just a jar you cannot see. A hairline crack empties overnight and then you have a dry pot and a smug weed. Cover the opening. Refill before it goes empty for long, so the clay does not dry and sulk.

## This pot is not a cup

Olla water is irrigation water. It is in contact with clay and soil and roots. Do not lift the lid and drink. Do not store your treated drinking water in a buried porous pot and call it a cistern. The cistern is in the dark cupboard, with a lid that means it.

Grey water — the wash water, not the toilet, never the toilet — is a separate argument, and this book will keep it short. If you use wash water on a garden at all, it stays off the leaves you eat, it is not full of salt, bleach, or a mysterious "grease-cutter," and it does not pond against the house. Soil contact and an olla are sometimes how people keep that water away from the salad itself. That is a caution, not a recommendation to water your lunch with the dishes. When in doubt, the olla gets the cleanest water you can spare for plants, and your mouth gets the water from chapter 8.

The zombie buries a glazed vase and waits for the miracle. The miracle was the pores. You checked for the pores before you dug the hole.
