# Weather Does Not Care About Your Backstory

You have escaped the horde, crossed the ridge and established that your waterproof jacket is an aspirational document. The weather has no interest in your character arc. It wants heat, water and exposed skin, and it is prepared to work overnight.

## Cold: warm the person, not your theory

Hypothermia is dangerous cooling of the body. Shivering, clumsiness, slurred speech, confusion, drowsiness or slow breathing in a cold-exposed person need urgent attention. Shivering may cease in severe hypothermia; that is not proof of recovery. Seek emergency help. [R16]

Move into shelter if safe, insulate from the ground, replace wet clothing gently with dry layers and cover the head and body. Handle a severely cold or confused person gently and minimise unnecessary exertion. Warm the trunk gradually with dry insulation and a warm environment. Do not rub limbs, use a hot bath, apply fierce direct heat or give alcohol. Offer a warm non-alcoholic drink and sugary food only if fully awake and swallowing safely. [R16]

Watch breathing. A cold person who becomes unresponsive needs the response and breathing assessment in Chapter 2 and emergency help; start CPR if breathing is abnormal. Do not assume death because someone is very cold and still. Lay triage shortcuts have no business here.

## Heat: confusion is an emergency

Heat exhaustion may cause heavy sweating, headache, nausea, dizziness, weakness and thirst. Stop activity, move to shade or a cool place, loosen clothing, cool the skin and offer suitable fluids if the person is alert and can swallow. Monitor their response. Heatstroke can involve confusion, collapse, seizure or very high temperature and is an emergency; sweating may still be present. [R33]

Call emergency help and start active cooling immediately: use cool water, wet cloths and fanning, removing unnecessary clothing. Place wrapped cold packs at the neck and armpits if available. Do not give a drink to a confused or unresponsive person or use fever tablets as treatment for heatstroke. A person not improving promptly with cooling needs urgent assessment. [R33]

Plan work for cooler periods, make shade, schedule rests and ensure access to water. Check each other, especially when someone is concentrating on a task. The confident person operating a shovel can be the last to notice that their brain has turned into porridge.

## Feet: the transport system with toenails

Inspect feet daily during sustained walking or wet conditions. Check heels, between toes, soles and pressure points. Clean and dry gently, change socks, remove grit and deal with rubbing early. Fit footwear to the actual foot and task, allowing for swelling. A blister ignored at breakfast can reorganise the expedition by supper.

Protect an intact blister with an appropriate dressing and avoid bursting it. If it opens, wash gently, preserve the remaining skin where possible, and cover. Increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus or spreading pain needs assessment. Diabetes, poor circulation or reduced sensation lowers the threshold for seeking help. Do not let a person with numb feet use pain as their only warning system.

Prolonged wet, cold exposure can injure feet even without freezing. Get them dry, warm gradually and avoid more exposure; numbness, swelling, unusual colour, severe pain or loss of function needs urgent advice. Do not rub damaged tissue or apply direct intense heat. Frozen-looking tissue also needs professional assessment and protection from further injury; avoid walking on it if alternatives exist. [R16, R29]

## Shelter is part of the prescription

Dry bedding, adequate ventilation, safe heating and enough food support recovery. Separate the sleeping area from cooking fumes, fuels and a generator. Never bring a barbecue or combustion heater intended for outdoors inside for warmth. A candle is not a medical device; use a stable, safe light and keep fire away from oxygen, bedding and loose clothing.

Make the shelter usable for the least mobile person, not only the fastest. A toilet reached over slippery steps is an injury programme. Place water, lights and call signals within reach. Check the person who stays quiet in bed: reduced mobility and cold can become a mutually reinforcing trap.

> FIELD RULE: Prepare for exposure before the patient gets cold, thirsty or confused. The best emergency blanket is often the dry layer you never allowed to get wet.

**Sources:** R16, R29, R33. Foot-care advice is general; seek individual advice for diabetes, circulation problems or sensory loss.
