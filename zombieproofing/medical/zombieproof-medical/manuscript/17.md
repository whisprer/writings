# Read This When Your Brain Has Left

These pages are reminders, not a replacement for the chapters or practical training. Read them while calm, keep them with the kit, and use current dispatcher or clinician instructions when available. The record sheets are intentionally blank tools for real observations; do not fill a gap with a guess. Their layouts are original for this edition, informed by R27, and are not official clinical forms.

## Card A: immediate danger and adult collapse

1. Check for hazards before approaching. Do not enter smoke, toxic atmospheres, unstable structures or live electrical danger.
2. Check response. If an adult is unresponsive, call 999 or 112 immediately on speaker; send a helper for an AED.
3. Open the airway and check normal breathing for no more than ten seconds. Gasping is abnormal.
4. If not breathing normally, start CPR: centre of chest, 5-6 cm deep, 100-120 per minute, full recoil.
5. Give 30 compressions to two breaths if trained and able; otherwise continuous compressions. Use the AED and follow prompts. Nobody touches during analysis or shock.
6. If breathing normally, protect the airway and keep checking. Use recovery position when appropriate; minimise movement with major trauma unless airway or safety requires it.

Children and babies need the initial rescue breaths and age-appropriate methods in Chapter 15. Continue care until help takes over, normal breathing returns, you cannot continue or the scene becomes unsafe. [R01-R04, R51]

## Card B: serious bleeding

1. Call emergency help and use firm direct pressure at the bleeding site with a dressing or clean cloth.
2. Do not remove embedded objects. Apply pressure around them without pushing them deeper.
3. For life-threatening arm or leg bleeding not controlled by pressure, use a commercial tourniquet according to training and its instructions. Tighten until bleeding stops, record time and do not release it.
4. Keep the person warm, still and monitored; recheck after movement. Give nothing by mouth if drowsy or swallowing is unsafe.
5. Pursue urgent handover. Do not stitch, cauterise or perform invasive procedures from this book. [R03, R20]

## Card C: thermal burn

1. Make the scene safe and stop the burning process.
2. Cool the burn with cool or lukewarm running water for twenty minutes. Keep the rest of the person warm.
3. Remove nearby jewellery and loose clothing; leave stuck material in place.
4. Cover loosely after cooling. No ice, butter, creams or tight wrapping; do not burst blisters.
5. Seek urgent assessment for major, deep, chemical or electrical burns, vulnerable locations, illness or breathing concerns. Chemical exposure requires specific advice. [R08, R09]

## Card D: suspected anaphylaxis

1. Rapid breathing difficulty, throat/tongue swelling, faintness or collapse after a possible trigger is an emergency. A rash can be absent.
2. Use the person's adrenaline auto-injector promptly according to its own instructions. Call 999 and say anaphylaxis.
3. Keep lying down; if breathing is difficult, allow sitting up slowly with legs extended. Do not let them stand or walk.
4. If not improved after five minutes, use a second auto-injector if available.
5. Monitor. Start CPR if unresponsive with abnormal breathing. Antihistamines do not replace adrenaline. [R26]

## Card E: diarrhoea, dehydration and infection

1. Offer frequent small sips only if awake and swallowing safely. Use suitable ORS mixed with the exact water volume on the sachet.
2. Watch intake, urine, alertness, breathing, vomiting and diarrhoea. Continue appropriate feeding and seek early advice for children or vulnerable adults.
3. Urgent help: unable to keep fluids down, markedly reduced urine, blood, severe pain or worsening illness.
4. Emergency help: confusion, difficult breathing, collapse, blue/grey or mottled skin, or difficulty waking.
5. Keep hands, safe drinking water, food and waste separated. Several cases require an outbreak check, not blame. [R11, R13, R14]

## Card F: before moving a patient

- Why move now, and is the destination able to help?
- Is the route safe, the equipment suitable, and the team sufficient?
- Is bleeding controlled as far as possible, breathing protected and the person insulated?
- Who coordinates the move, watches the patient and records changes?
- Are medicines, identification, care plans and records travelling with them?
- Who receives the person, and what happens if the route closes?

Recheck after every move. Do not move an injured person unnecessarily, but do not leave them in immediate danger merely to avoid movement. [R07, R22, R27]

## The final check before sleep

Make the next watch explicit. Confirm who has the patient, where the medicines are, what must be checked and when to call for help. Put the torch where the next person can find it. Restock the used dressing, not the imagined one. Write the information down.

The dead may walk. The living still need clean water, warm bedding, useful records and somebody who comes back to check. If the book has persuaded you to be that somebody, it has earned its space beside the bandages.
