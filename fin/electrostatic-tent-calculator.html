Physics model & assumptions

Charge generation (empirical): σ = σ_mat · (v/600 fpm)^0.5 · (μ/0.4)^0.8 · e^(−(RH−30)/45), clamped at the air-breakdown ceiling σ_max = ε₀·E_bd ≈ 26.6 µC/m² (E_bd ≈ 3 MV/m). σ_mat is a per-material triboelectric coefficient — tunable in code, not a handbook constant.

Tent field (exact 2-D): each web span (two walls + ceiling) is an infinite charged strip. Field of a strip spanning y₁..y₂ at perpendicular distance d: E⊥ = σ/(2πε₀)·[atan((y₂−y)/d) − atan((y₁−y)/d)], E∥ = σ/(4πε₀)·ln((d²+(y₁−y)²)/(d²+(y₂−y)²)). Contributions are summed as vectors. Film assumed negatively charged (PP sits negative in the triboelectric series); force magnitudes are sign-independent.

Force on a person — two models shown:

  • Electrostatic pressure (conductor in field): F = ½·ε₀·E²·A
  • Induced-dipole / gradient: F = ½·ε₀·α·∇(E²), α ≈ 3·V_body ≈ 0.225 m³ for a standing adult. This is the model that produces an actual “wall” — a force opposing motion toward the charged web.

Baseline cross-check: at σ ≈ 20 µC/m² the model gives ≈ 770 kV/m (≈ 235 kV/ft) at chest height mid-tent — consistent with Swenson’s 200 kV/ft electrometer being slammed to full scale, and with hair standing on the production manager. Predicted pushback is of order 1–2 N: modest in absolute terms, but delivered as an invisible, hair-raising, uniformly distributed pressure — enough to make a person instinctively refuse to lean further. Treat absolute force values as order-of-magnitude; the geometry/field trends are the reliable part.

“He could lean all his weight forward but was unable to pass… The ‘invisible wall’ effect had returned. He commented that he ‘didn’t know whether to fix it or sell tickets.’” — D. Swenson, 3M, EOS/ESD Symposium case study, 1995 (event: August 1980)
