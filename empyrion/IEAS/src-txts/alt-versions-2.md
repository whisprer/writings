The Ghost and The Hunter

Log Entry - Unregistered Traffic Detected

Timestamp: 05:17 UTCLocation: Drift Region Theta-9, near the edge of Inzaka-4’s gravitational haloAlert Level: HIGH

The Papa Samuel drifted through the dark void, its rust-streaked hull blending into the clutter of abandoned freighters and salvage debris along the outer perimeter of Inzaka-4. As always, the ship played the part of an outdated cargo vessel—slow, unassuming, and practically forgotten.

Inside, Captain Sal leaned over the navigation console, his fingers absently tapping out an old rhythm as he watched a new alert scroll across the terminal.

Unregistered vessel detected. Minimal emissions. Unknown trajectory.

He sighed, rolling his shoulders. The Falcon was docked and ready, its sleek form hidden behind false bulkheads. Whatever was out there, it wasn’t the usual cargo hauler.

The encrypted comm channel flared to life.

“Sal, you seeing this?” Barry’s voice came through, sharp and clipped. “That ship ain’t broadcasting anything standard. Looks military, but… different. You ever seen a signature like that before?”

Sal inhaled deeply, staring at the spectral outline on his scope. “I’ve seen plenty of ghosts, Barry.” He clicked his tongue. “This one? It’s hunting.”

The Aardvark Knows No Fear

Renna "Hex" Vostek was always ready for a fight. Her fingers flexed over the Aardvark's controls, heart steady despite the unmistakable tension in the air.

The stolen Levit8-AERO™ was out there. Somewhere. And now? Woflcorp had its eyes on the Papa Samuel. The Aardvark had been dispatched to neutralize all potential interference—including a certain smuggler crew that had been dodging authorities for far too long.

Hex smirked, flipping a switch.

“Engage full-scan mode,” she commanded. “Let’s see what’s hiding in the dark.”

Her co-pilot, Havik, frowned at the readings. “That freighter’s giving off old Gemini-class emissions, but something’s off. It’s got a ghost signature buried in the heat trail.”

Hex’s grin widened. “Oh, you sneaky bastards. You’ve got a runner inside that bucket, don’t you?”

She keyed the comms. “This is Woflcorp. Strike Division. Identify or be fired upon.”

Silence.

Then static. A voice broke through. Slow. Unbothered.

“This is Captain Sal of the Papa Samuel. You sure about that, Woflcorp?”

The Falcon Takes Flight

Inside the freighter, Barry was already moving. The Falcon hummed as its auxiliary power thrusters came online, burning hot inside Bay No. 19. Its transponder remained cold. Unreadable.

“They’ve got target locks,” Barry muttered. “We don’t move now, we won’t move at all.”

Sal exhaled sharply. “Fine. Do it.”

Barry slammed the release.

The Papa Samuel shuddered as the Falcon blasted free, its blackened hull vanishing into the void like a shadow breaking away from its source.

The Aardvark’s alarms flared to life.

“Contact—fast mover, launching from the freighter!” Havik barked. “Speed off the charts!”

Hex’s grin vanished. “That’s no bulk hauler.”

She grabbed the controls. “Aardvark to control—we’ve got a wild one. Engaging pursuit.”

The Chase Through the Dark

The Falcon weaved between drifting wreckage, its frame cutting through the void in bursts of rapid acceleration. It had no weapons, but it didn’t need them. It was fast.

The Aardvark, however, wasn’t built for subtlety. Hex punched forward, thrusters screaming as railguns primed for a precision shot.

“Lock that bastard down!”

Havik worked the targeting systems, sweat beading along his brow. “They’re threading through debris—can’t get a clear shot!”

Barry gritted his teeth inside the Falcon. “Oh, you think you’re fast?”

He flipped a hidden switch. Warp capacitor online.

The Falcon blurred for half a second. Then it was gone.

The Ghost Vanishes

Hex swore as her scanners lost the signature. The Falcon had disappeared, cutting between sensor gaps so tight they were practically invisible.

“Son of a—”

Havik slammed a fist against the console. “They jumped, but it wasn’t standard warp. That was something else.”

Hex exhaled sharply. “That ship isn’t just a smuggler’s toy.”

She switched comms. “Command, this is Vostek. The runner’s gone, but we’re marking that freighter. They’re hiding something bigger.”

A pause.

Then the cold, professional voice of Woflcorp’s central command.

“Understood. Track the Papa Samuel. If they slip again, burn them.”

Hex grinned. “With pleasure.”

Elsewhere, in the Shadows

Barry exhaled as the Falcon settled into silent running, deep in the shadow of a drifting asteroid.

“That was close.”

Sal’s voice came through, calm as ever. “Too close.”

Barry checked the nav data. “We can’t stay hidden forever. Woflcorp knows we’re more than just a rust bucket now.”

Sal nodded. “Yeah.” He stared out at the stars, his fingers brushing over an old charm around his neck.

“Which means it’s time to stop running.”

Barry blinked. “Come again?”

Sal turned, eyes burning with something fierce.

“We’re gonna take the fight to them.”
