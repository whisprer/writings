"Ghost of the Fire Rain" — The I/EAS-3lectron Shogun's Last Mission
═════════════
The Shogun had already cemented itself in history with its audacious role in the Ares-class battleship heist, but that was only the beginning of its legend.

Some ships fade into obscurity after a single great mission—relegated to the dusty archives of corporate black-ops. But not the Shogun. It was a vessel designed to cheat fate itself, a ship that defied physics, logic, and the natural order of battle.

And so, it found itself deployed once more—this time into the heart of a celestial nightmare: Firerain-9, a death-world locked in an endless hellstorm of molten debris, electrical surges, and erratic magnetic pulses that fried most electronics on entry.

The objective was simple on paper: extract a high-value asset from the surface—a rogue scientist by the name of Dr. Halbrecht Reznov, a former woflcorp. engineer who had defected with experimental shield tech, vanishing into Firerain-9’s ash-choked skies.

But no mission was ever simple.

The Descent: Dancing with Death
The Shogun plunged through the swirling storms like a wraith, its engines flaring blue against the backdrop of crackling crimson fire. The ship’s composite structure creaked as it endured pressures no sane pilot would tolerate.

Inside, the pilot, Siv Korr, gritted his teeth, flipping through manual override switches as the Shogun's shields buckled under the storm’s raw energy. Beside him, his ‘navigator,’ Kex Veyna, was deep into the sensor suite, rerouting auxiliary power into shielding capacitors.

“Field strength’s dropping past safe!” Kex called over the turbulence.

Siv didn't answer immediately—his hands blurred over the controls, bringing the Shogun into a near-vertical dive to ride a magnetic updraft, skimming the infernal clouds instead of fighting through them. A standard I/EAS shuttle would have been atomized in seconds, but this wasn’t a standard shuttle. The Shogun was engineered for reckless miracles.

“Hold on, I’m threading the gap—” Siv muttered, the ship rattling violently as a streak of plasma narrowly missed them, casting an eerie glow through the cockpit glass.

Kex's console blared a proximity warning. Incoming bogies.

The Intercept: Black Dagger Strikes
Before the mission, Intelligence had briefed them that Reznov was alone. That was a lie.

The Shogun’s long-range sensors picked up an unmarked Black Dagger gunship lurking in the storm. Black Dagger—woflcorp.'s ruthless, off-the-books enforcers. If they were here, it meant one thing:

Reznov wasn’t being extracted. He was being eliminated.

"Bogies on approach, vector 3-1-9—they know we’re here."

"Figures," Siv sighed, punching up the Shogun’s countermeasures.

As soon as the gunship locked onto them, Shogun spun mid-plummet, flipping to bring its twin railguns online. Kex armed the pulse laser turrets as the enemy ship fired a spread of magnetically guided missiles, the storm's electrical fields making their trajectories unpredictable.

"Shogun—firing!"

The railguns cracked through the storm, spearing through the void just as Siv inverted the ship to dive past a molten debris field. The first slug grazed the Black Dagger gunship’s hull, while the second ripped through its left engine nacelle, sending it into a wild spin.

The storm took care of the rest.

"That’s one way to clean up," Kex smirked.

The Extraction: Reznov’s Last Play
With no more opposition, the Shogun blasted through the lower atmosphere and zeroed in on Reznov’s compound, which was barely standing amidst the molten wasteland.

Reznov had a choice: come with them or be left to die.

He took neither. Instead, he handed Kex a small data core, wiped clean of any woflcorp. tracking signatures.

“You want the real shield tech, right?” Reznov grinned, knowing full well they had been sent to recover him.

Siv frowned. “You’re not coming?”

Reznov simply shook his head. “I have unfinished work. Take that to who needs it. Leave me to mine.”

The storms were intensifying. They didn’t have time to argue. With a curt nod, Siv and Kex boarded the Shogun, leaving Reznov in the ruins of Firerain-9.

The Escape: The Ghost in the Storm
On ascent, something changed.

Kex’s console lit up—another Black Dagger squadron inbound.

Four ships. Closing fast.

Shogun’s power reserves were running low, the shields flickering dangerously. But Siv wasn’t about to play fair.

“You ever heard of the Ghost Protocol?” Siv asked, flipping a hidden switch beneath his console.

Kex frowned. “Please don’t tell me you just—”

A sudden energy surge pulsed through the ship, overclocking its shield generators beyond safe limits. The Shogun phased in and out of sensor range, the ship briefly becoming a ghost on the battlefield—a trick stolen from Reznov’s research.

The Black Dagger ships lost their lock.

And before they could re-acquire a target, the Shogun was gone, lost in the infernal storm, leaving nothing but ionized echoes in its wake.

Reznov’s shield technology wasn’t just theoretical anymore. It was real. And it had just saved them.
