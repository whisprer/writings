INTRODUCTION: THE SHADOWS OF THE MEMUMOTH INCIDENT
The Memumoth Incident remains one of the most infamous operations in WoflCorp history. What should have been a routine freelance salvage op turned into a high-stakes heist when an unknown vessel—seemingly of human origin yet built with anomalous, near-alien hybrid technologies—was discovered drifting in a high-orbit debris field above Memumoth Prime.

The comms from the intercept crew tell a story of confusion, excitement, and sheer disbelief. The ship, though clearly built for human operation, defied conventional physics. Its structural framework was purely engine-based—eschewing the monocoque hulls of most standard SVs in favor of an advanced aerodynamic plate system known as Hybrid-Aero™ 'Aardvark' Ablative Armour.

And then there was the performance.

Whoever built this thing—whether it was black-market engineers, a rogue faction of the USNC, or something worse—they had mastered the art of speed.

It wasn’t just a fast ship. It was the fastest ship anyone had ever seen.

The I/EAS "Aardvark", as it was later designated by WoflCorp R&D, was designed for emergency extractions, high-speed infiltration, and the kind of dangerous operations where leaving the scene at supersonic speeds was just as important as getting in undetected. It boasted unparalleled handling in extreme conditions: high gravity, dust storms, dense atmospheric interference—it was a machine built for the impossible.

Following the Memumoth Incident, WoflCorp wasted no time reverse-engineering and adapting the stolen vessel into a classified production line. The Hybrid-Aero™ armor and engine systems were the most valuable discoveries—tech unlike anything else in known space.

The ship was designated I/EAS "Aardvark", a nod to its peculiar, almost predatory aerodynamic design, as well as its role in burrowing deep into enemy-controlled environments for high-stakes extractions.

While the original ship remains classified under WoflCorp R&D, modified production versions of the Aardvark have entered specialized service—though only the most elite operators have been granted access.
