The Last Miner of Outpost 03a
I. The Silent Rig
The sky above Ascalon IV was an unbroken sheet of gray, dust storms sweeping across the horizon. This was no place for a holiday—no lush forests, no cool oceans, no breathable air without a filtration mask. But for Karsten Voss, this was home. Or at least, as close to home as he’d ever had in the past five years.

His mining platform—Outpost 03a—stood alone in the middle of a barren plain, reinforced concrete and steel dug deep into the cracked red soil. It wasn’t much, but it was his. A Woflcorp-issue compact rig, built for one autominer, a teleporter, and enough defenses to keep the occasional raiding party or stray drones at bay.

The platform had a 200-hour uptime on solar, and Karsten was meticulous about keeping it running efficiently. The generator boost was set to only kick in when the teleporter activated, minimizing fuel usage. The autominer drilled away in its armored enclosure, separating rich deposits of tungsten from the rock below, sending it up through conveyor chutes for storage. It was all routine. Monotonous.

Until today.

Today, something had tripped the external sentry gun, and Karsten was alone out here.

II. Ghosts in the Sand
He adjusted his visor, tapping the holo-display on his left wrist.

SENTRY GUN REPORT:
Motion detected - Quadrant 3
Target unidentified - No engagement.

Karsten frowned. The sentry gun had seen something but hadn’t fired. That usually meant biological movement, but there was nothing alive on Ascalon IV—just rocks, dust, and the occasional off-world scavenger.

He climbed up the platform’s observation tower, one hand resting on the sniper rifle holstered at his hip. From here, he had a clear view of the terrain. To the west, the distant lights of a passing freighter flickered in the sky—probably a Woflcorp supply run to a deeper mining facility.

Then, he saw it.

Footprints.

A long trail of deep, erratic prints leading toward the base, only stopping twenty meters from the main ramp.

But there was no one there.

He unslung his rifle, scoping the area. The thermal readout showed nothing. No heat signatures, no movement. Just the marks in the dust where something—or someone—had walked up to his platform… and disappeared.

His gut twisted.

"Who's out there?" he called out, his voice swallowed by the wind.

Nothing.

But something was out there.

III. Intrusion
A warning chime rang from his console inside.

Power Surge Detected – Internal Grid.

Karsten was already moving, sprinting down the metal stairs and bolting through the narrow entrance hatch. The corridor lights flickered, blue power conduits pulsing as the generator engaged.

The teleporter had activated.

Someone—or something—was inside.

His fingers gripped the pistol at his belt as he rounded the corner, clearing the storage bay first. Nothing. The fuel depot—empty. Then, the teleporter chamber.

The platform was still shimmering, residual energy arcing along the metal frame. But the arrival pad was empty.

That was impossible.

His heart pounded. He turned toward the core housing, pushing open the reinforced blast door. Everything looked normal—the core and CPU extender sat safely behind its steel barriers. But his eyes locked onto something new:

A single handprint on the wall.

Smudged. Bare. Human.

And fresh.

He wasn’t alone.

IV. A Voice in the Dark
A whisper crackled through his comms.

"…you need to leave."

Karsten froze. The voice was faint, distorted, coming through an encrypted local frequency.

"Who is this?" he demanded. "Identify yourself!"

No response.

Instead, the sentry gun outside opened fire.

BANG-BANG-BANG.

Karsten cursed, sprinting up the hatch ladder to the external landing platform. From here, he had a clear view of the turret tracking something moving at impossible speeds, circling the base in erratic, unnatural jerks.

Then it stopped.

A figure stood just beyond the platform.

Not a raider.

Not a soldier.

A man.

His suit was torn, dust-covered, a mining badge still half-visible on his shoulder. His face was hidden behind a cracked visor, but the way he **stood—rigid, still, watching—**sent a deep chill through Karsten.

The sentry gun should have been ripping him apart. But it wasn’t firing.

And then Karsten understood.

Because the target scanner wasn’t marking him as alive.

The figure lifted a hand.

And the teleporter inside activated again.

V. The Truth Beneath
Karsten turned and ran, sprinting toward the console inside the base.

ACTIVE TELEPORT SIGNATURE DETECTED.
DESTINATION COORDINATES: UNKNOWN.

His mind raced. That was impossible. The 03a platform only had a single local teleport relay. It shouldn’t be able to connect to off-grid locations.

Unless...

Unless it was never his base to begin with.

His stomach dropped. He opened the security logs, scrolling back through the base records.

Then, he saw it.

Original Core Installation - 5 Years Ago.
Miner: Karsten Voss.
Core Transfer - 6 Months Ago.
Status: REVOKED.

His blood went cold.

Six months ago?

He never transferred the core.

Then who did?

His hands shook as he accessed the deeper records. The security feed rewound, static flickering before stabilizing on a single corrupted frame.

A man in a mining suit.

His mining suit.

Standing in the exact same position he was in now.

You need to leave.

Karsten staggered back, the world spinning. He turned toward the teleporter pad, heart hammering.

The same handprint.

The same footprints in the dust.

The same silent, watching figure outside.

And then—

The console flickered.

The base security protocols reset.

New Core Detected.
Welcome, Miner Voss.

His mouth went dry.

Somewhere outside, the figure was gone.

And he was alone again.

At least, for now.

Epilogue: The Core Cycle
Days later, Karsten tried shutting down the teleporter. It wouldn’t power off. No matter how much he rewired, reset, or bypassed, it reactivated.

The handprint on the wall? Gone.

The security logs? Wiped.

But the most disturbing part?

The footprints outside…

They were leading away from the base.

As if something had left.

And it was waiting for him to follow.

End.
