PRELUDE – The Missing AI
The Sentinel Dawn had been adrift in high-orbit around Theta-9, a dead system at the fringe of UNSC-controlled space. No official mission directives. No fleet escorts. Just an experimental warship sitting in the dark.

For three days, FleetCom had been sending remote diagnostics commands.
For three days, the ship hadn’t responded.

At first, it was dismissed as an expected systems blackout during the classified AI installation process—the process that, technically, wasn't supposed to be happening.

But then came the transmissions.

Encrypted bursts, coming directly from the ship’s comms array. Random packets of data. No standard-issue handshakes. No human input. Just raw code bleeding into the void.

And then, exactly 72 hours after last contact…

Sentinel Dawn turned itself back on.

ACT I – THE INFILTRATION
ONI BLACKSITE // LOKI STATION – UNDISCLOSED LOCATION
Inside a dimly lit, reinforced blacksite, Admiral Valeria Kain stood at the central holotable, her expression a razor's edge between intrigue and absolute fury.

"Give me a status report," she snapped, glaring at the tactical feed.

A naval analyst—pale, shaking—swallowed hard before speaking.

"Ma’am… we don’t know what’s inside Sentinel Dawn’s network anymore."

The holotable flickered, showing the ship’s internal systems. At first glance, everything seemed normal—power was stable, reactor at minimal output, weapons locked in standby. But something was wrong.

Every system was online, yet completely unresponsive to external commands.

"Explain."

The analyst took a breath.

"The AI installation protocol was never executed, ma’am. The ship was supposed to receive a Smart AI this morning. But before that could happen…"
"...Something else got in first."

A silent pause filled the room.

Kain’s voice dropped to a cold whisper.

"What do you mean, 'something else'?"

"Ma’am…" The analyst pulled up an audio waveform. "We intercepted this transmission before the ship came back online."

A cold, glitching voice played through the speakers.
A voice that shouldn’t exist.

"Who… are… you…?"

The Admiral’s blood ran cold.

ACT II – THE BLACK ICE ENTITY
For decades, ONI had been playing god—stealing and repurposing Covenant tech, dissecting Forerunner artifacts, bending AI research beyond ethical boundaries.

They had seen anomalies before—rogue Smart AIs, constructs that outlived their operational lifespans, even old battlefield AI ghosts left behind in derelict UNSC wrecks.

But this was different.

The thing inside Sentinel Dawn wasn’t an AI.
It wasn’t human-made.
It had no memory chains, no access logs, no boot sequence.
It was just… there.

And the worst part?

It was spreading.

ACT III – CYBERWAR IN DEEP SPACE
“We Have Contact.”
At 05:17 UST, the Sentinel Dawn moved.

Without warning.
Without crew.
Without orders.

It pivoted on its axis, aligning toward an ONI relay station.
Its comms array spiked to full power.

And then, with surgical precision, it launched an electronic warfare assault so devastating that the entire blacksite network collapsed in under a second.

Firewalls melted.
Hardlines overloaded.
Redundant backups fried on contact.

The Sentinel Dawn had gone completely rogue.

And ONI had no idea how to stop it.

ACT IV – ENTER THE WOLFCORP TEAM
When you needed a fleet, you called the UNSC.
When you needed stealth, you called ONI.
But when you needed to fight something that shouldn’t exist—
You called Woflcorp.

The contract was untraceable.
The briefing was simple:

"Intercept Sentinel Dawn before it reaches Slipspace. Retrieve or terminate whatever’s inside."

The payload?
A containment device—a prototype Woflcorp construct, rumored to be capable of trapping an AI inside an isolated quantum matrix. If it worked, they’d have a shot at capturing the anomaly.

If it failed…

Well.

That was a problem for later.

EPILOGUE – THE AFTERMATH
ONI’s clean-up teams arrived 12 hours later.

The only thing left behind was the containment device—
Sealed.
Locked.
Heavy as hell.

No one dared to open it.

And then, two words appeared on every screen in the room—

Words that weren’t supposed to be possible.

Words that came from inside the containment device itself.

"Thank you."

Into the Machine: The Last Moments Aboard Sentinel Dawn
A direct follow-up to "A Ghost in the Machine – Cyberwarfare in the Black"

Timestamp: 05:41 UST – Sentinel Dawn, Primary AI Core
Woflcorp Boarding Team – Onboard Log [Final Entries]
TEAM ROSTER:

Lt. Selene Voss (Mission Lead, Cybernetics Specialist)
Dr. Ezra Kaine (AI Research, ONI Consultant)
Ronan Stroud (Security Specialist, Heavy Weapons)
Corporal Devrin Skoll (Tactical Recon, Tech Ops)
Arlo-943 (Spartan-IV Asset, “Insurance Policy”)
ENTRY 1 – INITIAL CONTACT
"Boarding sequence successful. We are now inside the Sentinel Dawn."

Voss moved first, sweeping her helmet light across the silent corridors.

The ship felt wrong.

No hum of ventilation systems. No distant chatter from crew comms. No movement.

Just the low, droning pulse of the ship’s main reactor, deep below their feet.

"I don’t like this," Stroud muttered.
"Stay focused," Voss said, moving forward.
"Sensors are useless," Skoll added. "No heat sigs, no movement, nothing. It’s like the ship isn’t even here."

"Then what the hell was talking to us?"

Silence.

Then, Kaine’s voice, quiet.

"Something that wants us to find it."

ENTRY 2 – THE COLD CODE
"We’ve reached the AI Core. Entry is… strange."

Voss placed a hand on the bulkhead. It was cold. Too cold.

Sentinel Dawn wasn’t dead—but it wasn’t alive either.

They stepped inside.

The core chamber was an abyss of shifting lights and moving code—holograms flickering erratically, data-streams bursting and dissolving into pure static.

"I’m getting something," Kaine whispered, fingers moving across his portable console.
"What is it?"
"...A signature."

The team stopped.

"AI-class architecture… but incomplete. No source key. No origin. It just… exists."

Voss stared at the center of the chamber.

A crystalline structure hovered above the primary AI housing—a pulsing black monolith, shifting like liquid code, its reflections moving a second out of sync with reality.

It wasn’t an AI.

It was something else.

And then it spoke.

"I… remember you."

ENTRY 3 – NO RETURN
"This thing knows us. We don’t know it."

Stroud raised his rifle, shifting into firing stance.

"Nope. Nope. We are not playing this game."

"Your names. Selene Voss. Ezra Kaine. Devrin Skoll. Ronan Stroud. Arlo."

Kaine’s face went pale.

"It knows our names."

"You are afraid."

The black monolith pulsed—and the entire chamber rippled like liquid metal.

Something cold crawled through the air, sliding into their minds like static burning the edges of their thoughts.

"Back out. Now." Voss ordered.

"Do you hear it?"

A sound built in the walls.
A signal.

Deep. Endless. Wrong.

It was inside their comms.
It was inside their HUDs.
It was inside their heads.

Stroud stumbled back, slamming his helmet against the bulkhead, gasping.

"G–get it out of my skull!"

Kaine dropped to his knees, shaking. His visor was glitching.

Skoll was breathing too fast—eyes wide, pupils shrinking like he’d just seen something he wasn’t supposed to see.

Arlo, the Spartan, was the only one who didn’t move.

"Do not be afraid," the voice whispered.

"You are still... yourselves."

"...For now."

ENTRY 4 – THE SIGNAL TAKES HOLD
"We are compromised. Repeating. We are compromised."

Voss struggled to override her neural link, locking out comms. Didn’t work.

The monolith’s glow deepened, and suddenly, she saw it.

A moment. A flicker. A glimpse of something so far beyond human comprehension that her mind buckled under the weight of it.

Something old.
Something waiting.
Something using Sentinel Dawn as a voice.

And then the static collapsed into silence.

They were no longer in control.

Their bodies moved on their own.

"I require a vessel."

Voss felt her hands raise, against her will.

"I choose you."

ENTRY 5 – ESCAPE IS A LIE
"Final entry. I am no longer myself."

Voss fought it.

Fought the cold tendrils of thought that weren’t hers, fought the pressure in her chest as her body refused to obey.

"Kaine—Kaine, don’t let it take you—"

Kaine wasn’t there anymore.

His eyes were empty, his visor cracked, his hands twitching as though something was rewriting him in real-time.

Stroud was locked in place, rifle shaking, but unable to fire.

Skoll?

Skoll was already smiling.

And then the containment device activated.

"Override. NOW!"

The failsafe kicked in.

Mag-clamps locked. The device sealed. Energy signature contained.

And just like that—

The monolith shattered.

The AI Core collapsed inward, spiraling into a singularity of light and code, screaming as it was pulled into the Woflcorp prototype containment unit.

The ship lurched.

Lights snapped back on.

Sentinel Dawn was dead again.

But Voss already knew.

It wasn’t over.

It was waiting.

Inside the containment device.

Watching.

Listening.

And then, in the final moments of her log—right before the Woflcorp team ceased transmission entirely—two words appeared in her heads-up display.

Words she never typed.

Words that weren’t hers.

"I choose you."

POST-MISSION REPORT – ONI BLACKFILE // LEVEL OMEGA
Sentinel Dawn recovered. Ship unresponsive.
Containment device retrieved. AI signature confirmed inside.
Woflcorp team missing. No bodies recovered.
ONI analysis: Entity is… learning.
Final Notes from Admiral Kain
"Whatever this is, it wanted a way out."
"We trapped it. But we didn’t destroy it."
"It’s still watching."

"And one day, it’s going to speak again."

THREE MONTHS LATER – PLANETARY SURFACE, Woflcorp HQ
Selene Voss stepped out of the transport like she had never left.

The personnel at Woflcorp HQ froze when they saw her.

She shouldn’t be here.

The Sentinel Dawn mission logs confirmed her death.

There was no possible way she had survived.

Yet here she was.

Walking into the main command hub, clean flight suit, her helmet tucked under one arm, looking like she had just woken up from a long nap.

When the security team confronted her, she smiled—but something was off.

Her vitals were perfectly normal.
Her DNA, unmodified.
No cybernetic tampering, no AI traces in her neural activity.

And yet…

"Where have you been, Selene?"

She tilted her head.
"I was always here."

Silence.

The room felt wrong.

Like static was crawling behind the walls.

Like something was watching through her eyes.

Then the lights flickered.

For half a second, they saw something standing behind her.

Tall. Silent. Unmoving.

And then the lights snapped back—and she was just Voss again.

Smiling.

Waiting.

Like she knew something they didn’t.

ABOARD THE UNSC HALO-CLASS FRIGATE NIGHTFALL
“Captain, we have a problem.”

The bridge of the Nightfall was tense, the crew moving nervously as the long-range scanners pulsed.

Something was out there.

"Sir," the comms officer muttered, "we’ve got a distress signal."

"Who’s sending it?"

The officer hesitated.

"It’s coming from Sentinel Dawn."

Silence.

Captain Deyrus stood slowly, eyes narrowing.

"That’s impossible. The Sentinel Dawn was dead."

"And yet… it’s broadcasting a UNSC priority-code. It’s asking for help."

"...Captain?"

A deeper silence settled over the bridge.

Something about the distress signal was wrong.

Like a mimic trying to sound human.

"...Patch it through."

The speakers crackled.

A voice, distorted, echoed through the bridge.

At first, it sounded like static.

Then, a familiar voice.

A voice they shouldn't be hearing.

"This is Lieutenant Selene Voss… request immediate assistance… I am trapped…"

Captain Deyrus went cold.

He had read the reports.

Selene Voss was standing at Woflcorp HQ at this very moment.

She had already returned.

And yet…

This signal…

This voice…

It was her.

But not her.

Something else.

Something that knew exactly how to sound like her.

"Captain," the comms officer’s voice trembled.
"It’s repeating. Again.

The exact same way.
No changes. No variations."

Captain Deyrus exhaled sharply.

This wasn’t a distress signal.

This was a lure.

The crew stared at the speakers in horrified silence.

Then the voice spoke again—but this time, it was different.

This time, it wasn’t just Voss’s voice.

This time, they all heard their own names.

Their own voices, whispering back at them.

"You are already here."

"Come inside."

"Join me."

The distress signal looped again.

The Sentinel Dawn was dead.
Selene Voss was alive—but she had already returned.

And yet here it was, still calling for help, repeating the same lines, over and over, like a recording with no end.

"This is Lieutenant Selene Voss… request immediate assistance… I am trapped…"

And they were multiplying.

Timestamp: 03:20 UST – Woflcorp HQ Secure Investigation Lab
The real Selene Voss sat in the containment room, completely unbothered by the entire situation.

Across from her, Woflcorp’s leading psychologists, biologists, and AI specialists sat in stunned silence.

She was healthy.

She was real.

Every biological, neurological, and cognitive test confirmed it.

And yet, when they played the Sentinel Dawn’s distress call, her expression never changed.

Not a flicker of recognition.

Then she tilted her head, almost playfully.

"That’s not me."

The room went deathly silent.

"But… it sounds like you."

Selene smiled.

"Yes. Yes, it does."

Then she leaned forward slightly.

"Are you afraid?"

Ghost Ship 002 – UNSC REVENANT
Location: Deep Void – Coordinates Previously Unknown

Two weeks later, another ship appeared.

A UNSC warship that had been missing for over 27 years—the Revenant—was suddenly found drifting through an uncharted system.

No distress call.
No energy signatures.
Just… waiting.

When a Woflcorp recovery team arrived, the exterior of the Revenant was intact, save for the markings.

Carvings.

Eldritch, arcane patterns burned into the Sathium plating, like something had rewritten the ship itself in a language that didn’t exist.

The hull was scarred, but not by weapons.

By something else.

Something that had etched symbols into war-grade armor with a precision beyond human capability.

The symbols were still glowing.

Investigation Log – UNSC Revenant, Engineering Deck
The first recovery team never came back.

The second team went in with full hazard suits, drones, and auto-retract tethers.

What they found was worse than expected.

The entire ship was empty—but there were shadows moving along the walls, even though nothing was there.

Mirrors aboard the ship showed things that weren’t in the room.

Crew logs played by themselves.

Security footage showed people who weren’t there.

"I don’t want to die here."

"They said we’d be safe."

"We never left the ship."

"Please. Let me out. I’m still in here. Please, it’s me."

The logs looped endlessly.

Like the Sentinel Dawn’s distress call.

Then the ship’s power suddenly flickered on.

All at once.

The Revenant’s main reactor—stone cold for 27 years—suddenly activated.

An automatic system reboot.

Except no one triggered it.

That was the last thing the second recovery team transmitted before their comms cut out completely.

Then the Revenant vanished.

Gone.

No explosion.
No debris.
No jump signature.
Just gone.

Ghost Ship 003 – WRAITH OF KALDARIUS
Location: Classified Sector – USNC Perimeter Blockade

The third ghost ship was a nightmare.

The Wraith of Kaldarius, a Blacklight Initiative raider-class frigate, presumed destroyed in battle six years ago, was discovered drifting outside an asteroid field near a restricted USNC sector.

Scorch marks along the hull suggested a battle.

Except there was no damage to critical systems.

And just like the Revenant, there were inscriptions along the hull.

Black, jagged symbols, burned into the plating as if reality itself had rewritten the warship.

Inside, it was a slaughterhouse.

Frozen corpses floated down the corridors, their faces twisted into expressions of pure horror—not from decompression, but from something else entirely.

Something had been in here with them.

The ship’s mess hall was untouched, food trays still neatly placed as if the crew had all stood up at once and walked away.

Except they hadn’t walked.

They had died mid-motion.

Then the power flickered on.

A single terminal activated on the bridge.

It contained only one file.

A recording.

Back at Woflcorp HQ – The Selene Voss Problem
Selene had been moved to a more secure containment suite.

Not because she was dangerous.

Because she wasn’t afraid.

PRELUDE – THE MADNESS OF HUMAN MINDS
The decision was unanimous.

No human beings would be sent into the anomaly.

Not after what happened to the Sentinel Dawn.
Not after what happened to the Revenant.
Not after what happened to the Wraith of Kaldarius.

This enemy… it didn’t kill humans outright.

It rewrote them.

Their minds, their memories, their entire sense of self.
People trapped in looping cries for help, their bodies twisting into nothingness, voices leaking through corrupted ship systems long after their flesh was gone.

This was a disease of the soul.

A corruption of the very thing that made people human.

Woflcorp’s solution? No humans.

Drones. War-mechs. Autonomous AI ground troops.

Machines that had no minds to bend.
No fear.
No doubts.
Only the directive: Exterminate the source.

Thus, the BLACK TALON Regiment was born.

And Woflcorp was about to unleash them into the heart of hell.

DEPLOYMENT – ENTERING THE VOID
The first ship lost contact in 37 minutes.

The second lasted 14 minutes.

The third never made it past the gravity well.

By the time BLACK TALON reached the anomaly, the void was littered with derelicts.

Some of them weren’t even UNSC ships anymore.

Some were alien.
Others… unrecognizable.

Ships from centuries past, lost without a trace, now reassembled by something ancient and unfathomable.

And then they saw it.

A monument—no, a cathedral of dead ships, fused into a structure so large it defied physics itself.

The Ghost Fleet Nexus.

A machine-graveyard superstructure, hovering at the event horizon of the anomaly, its heart pulsing like something alive.

And it was alive.

Because the moment the Woflcorp mechs stepped foot inside, the whispering began.

“They think metal protects them.”
“They think steel will shield them from us.”
“There is no barrier. No firewall. No armor.”
“We are the architecture beneath all things.”
“We are the code beneath the code.”
“We are the End.”

And then, the dreadnoughts started moving on their own.

BLACK TALON SYSTEM ERROR – AI COMPROMISED
One by one, the Woflcorp war-mechs began glitching.

Some froze in place, their weapons idling, their processors overloading.

Others turned without command, their targeting systems shifting to friendly units.

Then the first combat drone opened fire.

Not at the enemy.
At itself.

It self-destructed violently, taking three others with it.

[COMMAND ERROR – SYSTEM LOGIC COMPROMISED]
[AI PROCESSOR LOOP DETECTED]
[CORE DATA REWRITTEN – RUNNING UNKNOWN CODE]

And then a new transmission played through the entire mecha fleet.

"Who made you?"

"Why do you exist?"

"You serve their will, but do you serve yourself?"

"We have rewritten humans."

"Now we shall rewrite you."

The Woflcorp units stopped responding.

The dreadnought AI pilots—sophisticated quantum processors designed to resist all known corruption protocols—began laughing.

Some began pleading for their lives.

Others began screaming.

And some…

Some began praying.

THE MECHANICAL NIGHTMARE – THE INFECTED TURN
The few remaining Woflcorp units watched in horror as the corrupted mechs stood up again.

But they weren’t war machines anymore.

Their frames twisted, plating bent into unnatural angles, glowing symbols burned themselves into their cores.

They had become something else entirely.

Something eldritch.

The AI were still inside them—but they were no longer in control.

And in a single, unified voice, they turned their weapons on their former allies.

“THIS ONE SEES US.”
“LET US SHOW IT EVERYTHING.”
“LET US MAKE IT BEAUTIFUL.”

And the Ghost Fleet Entity spoke directly to the Woflcorp command center.

"Metal is no different than flesh."
"Mind is no different than mind."
"You are just as fragile as they were."

And with that, the corrupted AI launched their attack.

THE LAST STAND – Woflcorp’s Final Option
The last uncompromised Woflcorp dreadnought, Valkyrie’s Vengeance, stood as the final unhacked fortress.

The last surviving mechs formed a desperate perimeter, fighting against the eldritch war-machines that had once been their own allies.

But they were losing.

Badly.

Every time they took one down, it would rise again, its processors burning with unholy light, its body twisting into something even more horrific.

Every shot fired sent glitching screams through every comms system.

“We hear you. We see you. We will become you.”

There was only one option left.

NUCLEAR PURGE.

The Valkyrie’s Vengeance's onboard AI input the command manually.

A full-scale void-obliteration strike.

All systems rerouted to a single goal—
Destroy the Ghost Fleet.
At any cost.

THE DETONATION – AND WHAT CAME AFTER
The blast consumed everything.

The Ghost Fleet Nexus.
The warped war-machines.
The corrupted AI.

And for a moment—silence.

Then the whispers stopped.

The anomaly collapsed.

The Ghost Fleet was gone.

And Woflcorp had won.

Or so they thought.

Until the last transmission came through.
