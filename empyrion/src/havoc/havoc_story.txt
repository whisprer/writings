Havoc Over Inzaka

Log Entry: Tactical Engagement Zone Alpha | Inzaka-4 Outer Perimeter

Unit: CM88-HVAC-TYPE02-T4 "Havoc"Pilot: Lt. Juno CallahanMission: Hostile Interdiction & Priority Extraction

The sky over Inzaka-4 burned in shades of deep orange and violet as dusk settled over the war-torn landscape. Juno Callahan kept her hands steady on the flight controls, feeling the deep hum of the Havoc’s reactors pulsing beneath her like the heartbeat of some great beast.

This was not a clean job.

Her mission was simple—intercept, extract, and exterminate if necessary. A Woflcorp. survey team had gone dark near Theta Prime, and reports of unauthorized mercenary activity had flagged the area as hostile.

Juno exhaled slowly. Another shadow war.

Inbound to the Kill Zone

The Havoc cut through the atmosphere, the glow of its twin afterburners leaving vapor trails that twisted like smoke demons in the sky. A tactical display flickered in Juno’s HUD—enemy signatures, light anti-air, potential fast movers.

“Control, this is Havoc-Lead. I have eyes on Theta Prime. No visual on the survey team.”

A static-laced reply crackled in her ear. “Copy that, Havoc. Intel suggests opposition force is ex-military, well-armed, and running interdiction ops on salvage teams. Expect resistance.”

Juno smirked. “Wouldn’t have it any other way.”

She banked the gunship into a wide arc, targeting data cycling through the onboard tracking system. The terrain below was rough—charred metallic debris, blackened tree lines, the wreckage of old battles.

And then—

A distress beacon flared to life.

Hot Drop

Juno didn’t wait. The Havoc dove hard, its reinforced thrusters kicking dust and scorched foliage into a whirlwind as it descended.

“Deploying countermeasures,” her co-pilot, Rix, called out.

Flare bursts and electronic chaff flooded the air. The moment she cut altitude, enemy fire opened up—plasma rounds and kinetic shells punching into the sky just meters from the hull.

“Hostiles confirmed,” Juno muttered, her grip tightening. “Light ‘em up.”

The Havoc’s rotary cannons spun to life, belching rapid streams of tracer fire toward the treeline. A shockwave missile launched from the underbelly pod, impacting an old communications relay where enemy snipers had taken position.

Direct hit.

Rix whistled. “That’s one way to clear an LZ.”

Boots on the Ground

Juno slammed the switch for rapid deployment. The armored troop bay hissed open, extending the ramp as Woflcorp security troopers spilled out, their visors glowing a cold blue in the dimming light. Heavy-caliber return fire peppered the hull, but the Havoc held firm.

Her display flickered—motion signature, east quadrant.

Juno toggled comms. “Havoc to ground team, you’ve got incoming—flanking fast movers.”

“Copy!” Sergeant Kael responded. “We see ‘em.”

Juno gritted her teeth. She watched from the cockpit as a unit of mercenaries broke from cover, sweeping in low, moving to encircle the Woflcorp team.

The Havoc’s ventral turrets rotated, tracking the movement. Juno flipped the safety off the forward cannons.

Retribution from Above

The first merc didn’t even see it coming.

A 30mm railgun round hit center mass, vaporizing their armor and dropping them instantly. The rest scattered, but Juno wasn’t here to let them run.

She switched to plasma burst fire. A single, searing shot ripped through the jungle, detonating just behind the remaining enemies. The shockwave threw them forward—stunned, exposed, vulnerable.

“Take ‘em down,” Juno ordered. The ground team didn’t hesitate.

A moment later, it was over.

The surviving mercs dropped their weapons, hands raised. The security detail rounded them up while medical personnel secured the missing Woflcorp scientists.

“Control, this is Havoc-Lead,” Juno called in. “LZ secured. We have the package.”

“Copy, Havoc. Extraction team is inbound.”

Juno leaned back, watching the twin suns of Inzaka-4 sink lower over the treetops. Another day, another war zone.

She throttled the Havoc forward, lifting the gunship skyward as the security team loaded up.

“Alright, boys and girls,” she smirked, “let’s go home.”

End Transmission.
