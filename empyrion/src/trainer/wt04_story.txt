"Wings Before War"
The canopy sealed with a sharp hiss, locking Rael inside the cockpit of the WT-04 Peregrine. The glass was crystal-clear, the kind of transparency that made you forget it was even there. It was just him, the controls, and the endless expanse of the training range below. A patchwork of rolling grasslands, jagged canyons, and shimmering ocean stretched beneath him, a playground of terrain meant to test every limit he had.

His instructor’s voice crackled in over the comms.

"Cadet Rael, callsign 'Striker'. Welcome to your final live test. Your objective is simple—engage and evade. Your weapons are hot, but you are not to kill your opponent. Tag them, force them to break, and survive. You do that, and the next time you fly, it won’t be in a trainer—it’ll be the real thing."

Rael flexed his hands against the control sticks, feeling the slight tension of the haptic feedback system pushing back against his grip. The Peregrine was a teaching tool, but it wasn’t a toy. If he mishandled it, it would let him know.

A warning tone pinged in his left ear.

"Bandit approaching, vector one-eight-zero. Altitude 3,000. Weapons free."

His eyes flicked to the HUD overlay, where a crimson arrow marked the incoming target. It wasn’t just another trainee—it was Captain Lanis, his instructor, the man who had spent the last six months drilling dogfight maneuvers into his skull.

"You're slow, cadet." Lanis’s voice was calm, unhurried. Rael knew what that meant.

He barely had time to roll left before a staccato burst of Gatling rounds ripped through the air where he had been. His thrust vectoring array kicked in, banking him hard as he punched the throttle, forcing a tight turn along the treetops of the valley below.

The Peregrine danced under his touch, but Lanis was a veteran, and he was right behind him.

"Predictable. You’re running. You don’t run from a fight."

Rael gritted his teeth. He had seconds to react before Lanis chewed through his shields. Think, think. Running wasn’t an option.

He cut the throttle, sending himself into a controlled stall. The sudden deceleration slammed him into the seat, G-forces clawing at his ribs, but it did exactly what he wanted. Lanis overshot, his own momentum throwing him forward past Rael’s position for a split second.

That was all he needed.

Rael flipped the Peregrine’s nose up, engines screaming, and let the Gatling cannons bark.

Direct hit.

"Hah!" Lanis’s voice had a note of amusement now. Not anger. Amusement.

Rael exhaled, but he didn’t let his hands relax just yet. He leveled off, pulling alongside Lanis, who was now flying parallel with him.

"You’re not bad, cadet."

Rael blinked. That wasn’t the voice of an instructor talking to a trainee. That was the voice of a squadron leader acknowledging one of his own.

"Come back to base. You pass."

The words barely registered. Rael just nodded, heart hammering as he turned toward the horizon.

For the first time, he felt like a real pilot.

And next time? He wouldn't be flying a trainer.

Next time, it would be for real.
